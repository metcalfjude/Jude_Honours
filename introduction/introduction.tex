\chapter*{Introduction}

% 1. The problem
The brain is among the most complex systems in science, and one of its most important features is its ability to build a rich understanding of the world from a small, selectively sampled fraction of the available input. Nowhere is this clearer than in vision. In humans, an estimated one in five cortical neurons is devoted to visual processing and control \cite{wandell_visual_2009, andrews_correlated_1997}, yet despite this computational power, the efficiency of information processed is extreme: for every \qty{1}{\mega\byte} of information reported by the retina, only about \qty{40}{\bit} are consciously processed \cite{ZHAOPING2026103211, kelly1962information, sziklai1956some}. The visual system must therefore be highly selective, and it achieves this by acting rather than passively receiving. High-acuity vision is confined to the fovea, so to build a useful representation of the visible world, an observer must move their eyes, directing the fovea towards successive locations of interest. These rapid ballistic movements, called saccades, organise periods of relative stillness known as fixations during which the brain gathers visual information, and together they form a sequence of decisions about where to look next, made several times every second during searching \cite{erkelens_coordination_2006, engbert_integrated_2011}. Each decision depends on what has already been seen, what is currently in view, and the goals of the observer. Eye movements are thus the external output of a closed perception--action loop in which the brain selects the very data on which it will subsequently make decisions about further perception. This process of decision-making informing further perception is called active vision.

% 2. Importance for neuroscience: gaze as an observable
This makes gaze an incredibly valuable observable for understanding the brain. Gaze trajectories provide a direct, low-dimensional, continuous, and non-invasive proxy for neural attention, so the spatiotemporal dynamics of attention become direct to measure \cite{kawashima_attentional_2022, spyropoulos_theta_2018, Bruce_2009_Saliency, chen_spatiotemporal_2022, boccignone_probabilistic_2016, hoffman_probabilistic_2006, wang_attention_2022, misic_active_nodate, ni_distributed_2024}. The dynamics of visual attention is driven by many neural processes that are otherwise difficult to access experimentally: the evaluation of stimulus salience \cite{Bruce_2009_Saliency, bujia_modeling_2022, huang_salicon_2015, cornia_predicting_2018}, working memory through inhibition of return (IoR) to explored visual regions \cite{ludwig_mechanism_2009, redden_inhibition_2021, van_ede_looking_2026, henderson_oculomotor_2012, klein_visual_2023}, as well as more generally in complex tasks integrating top-down goals \cite{kingstone_inhibition_1999, postle_selective_2006, schroth_eye_2025}, and the intrinsic timescales of neural activity through regions of visual processing and control (importantly the V1 and the superior colliculus)  \cite{tan_theta_2024, kawashima_attentional_2022, zubair_eye_2026, spyropoulos_theta_2018}. Even the low-amplitude movements made during fixation, once dismissed as noise, carry structure reflecting the underlying oculomotor and neural machinery \cite{ko_eye_2016, graham_ocular_2023, spauschus_origin_1999, otero-millan_fixational_2014, ko_eye_2016, ryle_simultaneous_2015, rucci_control_2015}, and are valuable in gathering information during fixation \cite{wu_fixational_2024, greschner_retinal_2002, bowers_effects_2019, roberts_fixational_2013, rucci_temporal_2018, rucci_unsteady_2015, rucci_miniature_2007, li_perceptual_2026}.

% 3. Importance for neuroscience: visual search as a proxy for neural attention
Visual search, in which an observer scans a scene for a target or simply explores it with a determined goal, is among the most demanding and informative settings for studying decision-making in the brain. The subject must balance an exploration--exploitation trade-off, deciding when to remain sampling promising regions and when to discover new ones, while avoiding wasteful re-exploration of depleted regions. This must be done efficiently and robustly within the brain, as the cost of neural computation is high and the brain must be trained to perform well to many tasks \cite{Gherri2011-wl, Li2026-oa, Wright1995-ji, harrison_cost_2023, mangun_sustained_1998}. The resulting trajectories which are found to be most efficient are rich and structured, with characteristic spatial statistics, temporal correlations, and preferred timescales \cite{naher_primate_saccades, chen_spatiotemporal_2022, chota_rhythmic_2018, spyropoulos_theta_2018, kawashima_attentional_2022, han_gamma_2022, rucci_temporal_2018}. Understanding the mechanisms behind them offers a window not only onto vision, but onto the general strategies the brain uses to explore, sample, and decide under uncertainty \cite{renninger_where_2007, knill_bayesian_2004, orban_neural_2016, delacour_neural_1997, terada_chaotic_2024, qi_fractional_2022, dong_neural_nodate}, such as has been developed under the theory of active inference \cite{friston_sophisticated_2021, parr_active_2022, Parr2025-xh}. Such efficient and structured dynamics also outline the importance of deficiencies in eye-movement patterns associated with a range of neurological and psychiatric conditions \cite{papanikolaou_levy_2024, caldani_saccadic_2017, morita_eye_2017, zhu_diagnosis_2024, law_eye-tracking_1973, lindner_disorders_2005, bittencourt_saccadic_2013, benitezy_eyetracking_1970}. This finding imbues these measurements with a broad value in science, towards informing biomarkers of global brain function that aggregate many regions involved in visual search.

% 4. Importance for physics, abstract sampling and nonequilibrium exploration of a dynamical goal
The rich structure of gaze trajectories now invites a suitably complex physical description. Gaze can be modelled as a patterned stochastic process which has been aggregated from a complex adaptive system in the network of neural activity in a critical regime with memory effects \cite{qi_fractional_2022, chen_spatiotemporal_2022}. Interpreting these dynamics within the informative visual landscape optimising a target search is the driving motivation for this thesis. This model makes it possible to ask quantitative questions about gaze dynamics: how memory effects and relative adaptive timescales improve dynamical sampling, the structure of the heavy-tailed statistics of noise-driven motion, and what enforces characteristic sampling timescales. Methods from statistical physics, nonlinear dynamical systems, and non-equilibrium thermodynamics have already provided new ways to characterise and explain the collective and dynamical properties of neural computation [CITE], and they offer a language for computational neursoscience that is both explanatory and precise; such a well-defined mathematical model then permits an understanding of the system which drives these dynamics, the attention mechanism of the brain itself. Gaze is also a rare example of a driven, history-dependent stochastic system for which high-resolution data are readily available, making it a natural testbed for such theories.

% 5. Existing approaches: classical, scale-by-scale
Existing work has approached gaze dynamics largely one scale at a time, both in terms of space and time. Studies of large-scale saccades characterise their timing, amplitudes, and kinematics \cite{erkelens_coordination_2006, ludwig_mechanism_2009, rucci_control_2015}. At the scale of whole search trajectories, stochastic models describe global strategies with heavy-tailed, Lévy-like exploration \cite{NIPS2017_194cf6c2, blazejczyk_stochastic_2021, lencastre_dynamical_2025}, or else through global information-maximising strategies \cite{cornia_predicting_2018, bremer_predicting_2021, Ruarte2024.11.29.626088, huang_salicon_2015, kummerer_deepgaze_2022, kummerer_understanding_2017, bhandari_modeling_2025, chen_adaptive_2021, cornia_deep_2016, kittler_effective_2026}. The faster- and shorter spatiotemporal scales of fixational eye movements are then treated as a separate subject, through stochastic descriptions of their high-frequency noise statistics \cite{engbert_integrated_2011, vasudevan_stochastic_nodate, kuang_temporal_2012} or through their perceptual role in rapid information-gathering \cite{martinez-conde_role_2004, otero-millan_fixational_2014}, as well as the low-amplitude drifts during fixation \cite{ko_eye_2016, ryle_simultaneous_2015, rucci_control_2015, martinez-conde_role_2004}. The various timescales of attention more broadly, such as theta rhythmicity, form a further literature rooted in neural recordings and behaviour \cite{tan_theta_2024, kawashima_attentional_2022, spyropoulos_theta_2018}. Each account is valuable on its own terms, but they are developed with different formalisms, different data, and often different assumptions about what the eye is doing.

% 6. Existing approaches: machine learning
Alongside these, the dominant recent approach to modelling human gaze comes from machine learning. A large body of work trains deep networks to predict either gaze trajectories \cite{bhandari_modeling_2025, wu_gitnet_2026, abdelrahman_deep_2026, bremer_predicting_2021, ozdel_gaze-guided_2024, akinyelu_convolutional_2022} or saliency maps indicating where observers are likely to look \cite{kummerer_modeling_2025, cornia_deep_2016, cornia_predicting_2018, kammerer2016deepgaze2, huang_salicon_2015, bujia_modeling_2022}. Most notably, DeepGaze III \cite{kummerer_deepgaze_2022} predicts human fixation locations with high accuracy given the image, the fixation history, and systematic biases in how people search \cite{kummerer_deepgaze_2022, kummerer_modeling_2025, dagostino2025what}. Such models have been used to test hypotheses about search, showing for example that simple low-level contrast is often sufficient to predict fixations \cite{kummerer_understanding_2017}, and that late fixations become dominated by intrinsic gaze biases and decouple from image content \cite{kummerer_deepgaze_2022}. Yet these networks are optimised to predict where the next fixation will fall, not why gaze behaves as it does. Their internal representations are difficult to interpret, they do not readily connect to biological mechanisms, and they say little about the temporal organisation of search as a dynamical process [CITE].

% 7. The gap
Each of these approaches leaves a gap; both between biological realistic accounts and empirical accuracy, and between the fine and coarse scales of perception which should each be captured under a single theory. Saccades and fixations are not independent processes: the regularity, size, and duration of saccades are influenced strongly by information gathered during fixation [CITE]. Models built for a single scale cannot capture this coupling, and their parameters are fitted separately and carry no common physical interpretation. As such, one cannot simply combine such theories into a patchwork theory, as this will not accurately describe the neural control of gaze dynamics. The combined large- and small-scale movements are also rarely measured together, so there are few quantitative constraints on how a unified model should behave across scales \cite{naher_primate_saccades}. As a result, we lack a single, biologically motivated model in which the heavy-tailed spatial statistics of search, the rhythmic timing of saccades, memory of explored regions, and the structured dynamics of fixation arise from the same underlying biologically-informed mechanism. As such, a unified mechanistic account of search dynamics, going beyond fixation prediction, remains largely unexplored.

% 8. The aim of this thesis
Such an account must reconcile the phenomena above while remaining biologically plausible, parsimonious, and capable of generating testable predictions about novel or unexplained aspects of visual search. It should treat these features as different aspects of one stochastic, history-dependent process rather than as separate phenomena patched together. This thesis aims to unify these behaviours within a single functional, dynamical model of human visual search, linking the statistics of gaze to the principles by which the brain actively samples its environment.
