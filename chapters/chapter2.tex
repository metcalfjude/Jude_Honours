\chapter{A Unifying Model}\label{chap:a_unifying_model}
% Eye movements during visual search have been thought to be a patchwork of distinct behaviours: nonlocal jumps (saccades), periods of fixation, and fine motion within fixations. This chapter presents a biologically motivated model which demonstrates that these behaviours can arise from a single physical process, and to motivate each dynamical component from first principles rather than treating each scale separately. Uniquely, the presented model captures and predicts the rich nested oscillations and superdiffusion in visual searching dynamics; moreover these dynamical signatures of attentional timescales, gamma-frequency (\qtyrange{40}{100}{\hertz}) sampling, and superdiffusion take origin from network-level neural activity patterns which have been found in the brain \cite{harris2026-cn}. Inspired by such neural dynamics, we model visual search trajectories as a stochastic process driven by travelling activity waves across the surface of the brain, using the theory of fractional neural sampling (FNS) \cite{qi_fractional_2022, chen_spatiotemporal_2022}.

We develop a physical theory of gaze which integrates informational awareness and accurate consideration of the statistics of search dynamics. The chapter is organised as follows: \cref{sec:model_setup} defines the model based from FNS dynamics before presenting the search task used as the paradigm throughout this work. \cref{sec:dynamical-information_adaptation} introduces the dynamical-informative kernel which is novel to this work, giving its modelling motivations (\cref{subsec:motivations}) and the regular information-driven temporal structure of transitions between regions of interest as they are explored through informational kernel deposition (\cref{subsec:adaptive_kernel_dynamics}). \cref{sec:local_harmonic_oscillations} derives and quantifies the prediction from neural dynamics that localised trajectories produce high-frequency simple harmonic oscillations. \cref{sec:levy_superdiffusion} treats the defining characteristic of FNS dynamics, Lévy superdiffusion, deriving the short-time noise-driven scaling of fractional absolute moments and the monoscaling of self-similar superdiffusion which the model presents. Comparison of each of these dynamics with eye-tracking data is deferred to the next chapter.

In summary, this chapter presents three key dynamical components of the unifying model of gaze dynamics we present: a kernel which couples regular information gathering with dynamical transitions, the high frequency oscillations which drive fine spatial dynamics, and superdiffusive spatial dynamics which are defined by a single exponent across all spatial scales. In \cref{sec:model_setup} we compared the modelled setup with an empirical experiment performed in \cite{naher_primate_saccades}. To examine this model as a valuable predictive and explanatory model of gaze dynamics, we compare to these human data.
