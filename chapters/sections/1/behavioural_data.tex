\section{Behavioural data}
\label{sec:behavioural_data}
%To ground the physical background of this thesis, the present section reviews the physical system we aim to model: the eye-tracking signal which characterises human visual attention. During active vision, the visual system does not operate as a passive receiver of sensory input, but rather as an active, dynamic sampler of a changing environment \cite{friston_does_2018, friston_sophisticated_2021}. This active sensing manifests across two coupled physical observables: the internal dynamics of rich neural oscillatory networks, and the external, mechanical execution of gaze trajectories. Crucially, these domains inform and drive each other, and each exhibit complex, scale-free dynamics in their activity. Characterised by background spectra with power law tails and anomalous diffusion, the visual system is capable of devoting attention to many spatiotemporal scales of event. Viewing these biological phenomena through the lens of statistical mechanics provides a rigorous foundation for mathematically modelling the visual system. This section establishes these empirical constraints by first examining the functional role of neural frequency bands and theta-gamma coupling (\cref{subsec:neur_freq_bands}), before detailing the multi-scale, stochastic regimes of human gaze trajectories (\cref{subsec:gaze_traj}). Together, these behavioural patterns motivate the fractional dynamics given by the FNS equations of motion, and the developments upon that model in the subsequent sections.
\subsection{Gaze trajectories}\label{subsec:gaze_traj}
Healthy human gaze trajectories are well characterised by a small set of dynamical patterns. The most studied pattern of gaze trajectories are their division into periods of fixation, interspersed by near-instantaneous jumps between fixations, these jumps being known as saccades \cite{erkelens_coordination_2006, seung_how_1996, lencastre_dynamical_2025, benitezy_eyetracking_1970}. Importantly, this behaviour of local movements interspersed with large discontinuous jumps suggest modelling eye movements through a Lévy flight. This paradigm has a limited history in the literature, and fitting data to a simple phenomenological Lévy flight parameterised by the stable parameter $\alpha$ has found some limited use as a biomarker for ADHD \cite{papanikolaou_levy_2024}. Broader saccade metrics have been found to be valuable as non-invasive biomarkers for broader neural disorders, including schizophrenia \cite{caldani_saccadic_2017, morita_eye_2017, zhu_diagnosis_2024, lindner_disorders_2005, zhu_diagnosis_2024}, autism \cite{bittencourt_saccadic_2013, wang_saccadic_2025}, OCD \cite{bittencourt_saccadic_2013}, bipolar disorder \cite{wang_saccadic_2025, bittencourt_saccadic_2013}, and Parkinson's disease \cite{wang_saccadic_2025, bittencourt_saccadic_2013}. The dynamics of fixation have received less scholarly attention as a biomarker of cognition. Gaze fixations exhibit three distinct dynamical patterns: near-instant microsaccades within the same point of focus, slow ocular drift about a point of focus, and high frequency ocular micro tremor \cite{van_ede_looking_2026,graham_ocular_2023, engbert_integrated_2011, spauschus_origin_1999}. Detailed examinations and systematic reviews of these behaviours have not considered their computational role in active sensing and efficient search. The work presented in this research strongly motivate a closer and more biologically informed understanding of the dynamics of gaze trajectories, across a number of spatial and temporal scales, to diagnose and understand the function of global functioning of the brain, much of which controls the visual system.
\subsection{Existing models of visual searching dynamics}\label{subsec:existing_models}
Progress towards modelling gaze dynamics during visual search spans physics, neuroscience, and machine learning. As such, existing models have been built with very different aims and methods. Cognitive and biologically inspired models explain where attention is drawn through biologically-inspired saliency maps and top-down guidance \cite{itti_model_1998, wolfe_guided_2021, Gayet2025-fv}. Normative models ask what an optimally informative searcher would do, selecting saccades by Bayesian belief updating and entropy reduction \cite{najemnik_optimal_2005}. Physics-inspired models may describe gaze through a dynamical system, through variational least-action principles \cite{NIPS2017_194cf6c2} or through stochastic descriptions of fixational drift \cite{engbert_integrated_2011}. More recently, deep networks trained on eye-tracking data predict human scanpaths on natural images with high accuracy \cite{kummerer_deepgaze_2022, abdelrahman_deep_2026}.

Each approach captures an important aspect of search, but so-far models must focus on a specific feature and scale of gaze dynamics. Most treat search as a sequence of discrete saccades and neglect continuous-time dynamics; fixational activity is modelled separately from saccades, and the most accurate predictors are not designed to be biologically interpretable. No model among those reviewed combines continuous-time dynamics, saccadic jumps, fixational motion, and an explicit informational account of search. The present model draws on three of these approaches: the information kernel of the ideal Bayesian searcher, the second-order saliency-driven dynamics of the least-action model, and the structured fixational motion of the self-avoiding walk. A full comparison of the most influential and successful models, along with a comparison of their core domains, aims, and key features, is given in \cref{tab:search_models_colored} in \cref{app:prior_models}.

\subsection{Timescales of neural computation}\label{subsec:neur_freq_bands}
Throughout much of this thesis, links are drawn between timescales of interactions and dynamics in the FNS model and well-studied frequency bands of activity in the human brain. It is then valuable to outline the studied computational and functional interpretations of such frequency bands. Neural firing activity is divided into frequency bands named by greek letters, originally discovered and decomposed from electroencephalogram (EEG) data. Divisions are only approximate, but for this thesis these bands are: delta (\SIrange{0.1}{3}{\hertz}), theta (\SIrange{3}{6}{\hertz}), alpha (\SIrange{6}{14}{\hertz}), beta (\SIrange{14}{30}{\hertz}), and gamma (\SIrange{40}{100}{\hertz}) \cite{fan_relation_2007, klimesch_frequency_2018}. Of particular interest to this thesis are the interpretations of theta and gamma neural activity as functionally important to neural computation; broadly, theta rhythms have been found to modulate and organise bursts in computational gamma-scale neural activity  \cite{qi_fractional_2022, fries_mechanism_2005, fries_rhythmic_2023, fries_rhythms_2015, spyropoulos_theta_2018, bastos_communication_2015}. Theta rhythms have been shown to modulate attention during auditory tasks \cite{wang_attention_2022, yang_neural_2024}, visual search tasks \cite{kawashima_attentional_2022, spyropoulos_theta_2018, Mo_competing_2019}, and when analysing the broad attentional mechanism of the brain \cite{tan_theta_2024, fan_relation_2007, klimesch_frequency_2018}. 

Theta-rhythmic cycles are finding strong interpretation in the cognitive neuroscience literature as organising and communicating information sampled from the environment for integration into the internal model of the brain \cite{fries_mechanism_2005, fries_rhythmic_2023, fries_rhythms_2015, spyropoulos_theta_2018, bastos_communication_2015}. Strong direct evidence for this theory include correlations between intelligence and high theta peak power in EEG data during tasks requiring focus and attention \cite{wang_attention_2022, tan_theta_2024}, and the discovered sensitivity of the visual system to react and search scenes on a theta rhythm \cite{kawashima_attentional_2022, spyropoulos_theta_2018}. Accordingly, the firing patterns which define the lowest level of information carried out in the brain are regularly found to be gamma in scale, with broadband signals being found within \SIrange{40}{100}{\hertz} in a wide range of measurements of neural activity \cite{canolty_high_2006, harris2026-cn, ursino_thetagamma_2024, brooks_thetagamma_2020, tort_thetagamma_2009}. The coupling between the phase of theta activity and the amplitude of gamma activity is known as theta-gamma coupling (TGC). This phenomenon, a specific form of phase-amplitude coupling (PAC), is schematically represented in \cref{fig:litrev-thetagamma_coupling}. The evidence and theory behind TGC are described in a systematic review by \textcite{ursino_thetagamma_2024}. This thesis provides a computation view of the function of TGC in the within the search dynamics of gaze as modulating periods of fixational attention during which information is dynamically sampled.
