\section{Gaze trajectories}
\label{sec:gaze_traj}
To ground the physical background of this thesis, the present section reviews the physical system we aim to model: the dynamics of gaze trajectories, which characterise human visual attention. While healthy human gaze trajectories are well characterised by a small set of dynamical patterns, the challenge lies in understanding their interactions, functional roles, and mechanistic origins. The clearest pattern of gaze trajectories is a division into: i) long jumps in positional attention, called saccades, interspersed with; ii) periods of relative stillness called fixations, during which local information about a visual scene is gathered \cite{erkelens_coordination_2006, seung_how_1996, lencastre_dynamical_2025, benitezy_eyetracking_1970}. Eye-tracking devices---such as the Eyelink 1000 used in collecting data for this thesis, as shown in \cref{fig:eye-tracking}\textbf{d)}---provide the high-precision measurements to analyse and model the gaze trajectories through continuous time, both during saccades and fixations.

Broader saccade metrics based on such jump processes are valuable as non-invasive biomarkers for broader neural disorders, including schizophrenia \cite{caldani_saccadic_2017, morita_eye_2017, zhu_diagnosis_2024, lindner_disorders_2005, zhu_diagnosis_2024}, autism \cite{bittencourt_saccadic_2013, wang_saccadic_2025}, ADHD \cite{papanikolaou_levy_2024}, and Parkinson's disease \cite{wang_saccadic_2025, bittencourt_saccadic_2013, kaski_ocular_2017}.

When the dynamics of fixation or gaze are considered, they are treated as separate subject matter, rarely treated together in single studies. Gaze fixations exhibit three distinct dynamical patterns: near-instant microsaccades within the same point of focus, slow ocular drift about a point of focus, and high-frequency ocular microtremor (OMT) \cite{van_ede_looking_2026,graham_ocular_2023, engbert_integrated_2011, spauschus_origin_1999}. There are several studies examining a rich physical model of fixational dynamics \cite{engbert_integrated_2011, engbert_normal, vasudevan_stochastic_nodate}, as well as developing the role such dynamics have in gathering information efficiently \cite{rucci_control_2015, rucci_miniature_2007, rucci_temporal_2018, rucci_unsteady_2015, roberts_fixational_2013, wu_fixational_2024}.

\subsection{Existing models of visual searching dynamics}\label{subsec:existing_models}
Progress towards modelling gaze dynamics during visual search spans physics, neuroscience, and machine learning. As such, existing models have been built with very different aims and methods. Cognitive and biologically inspired models explain where attention is drawn through biologically-inspired saliency maps and top-down guidance \cite{itti_model_1998, wolfe_guided_2021, Gayet2025-fv}. More normative models ask what an optimally informative searcher would do, selecting saccades by Bayesian belief updating and entropy reduction \cite{najemnik_optimal_2005}. We here examine in detail two influential models representing these two model-making paradigms: the self-avoiding random walk model of fixational dynamics presented in \cite{engbert_integrated_2011, Herrmann2017-hw}, and DeepGaze~III from \cite{kummerer_deepgaze_2022}, which utilises a deep learning model to predict saccade positions. A full comparison of a selection of influential and successful models, along with their core domains, aims, and key features, is provided in \cref{tab:search_models_colored} of \cref{app:prior_models}.

The self-avoiding random walk model \cite{engbert_integrated_2011} considers dynamics within a single quadratic potential that confines the gaze to a localised fixation. The gaze position is discretised on a square lattice, which is a potential surface. Visited positions increase the likelihood of introducing a self-avoidance condition, which relaxes on a short timescale. Of particular interest to this thesis is the interpretation of the explored potential as a motor map from the neurons in the superior colliculus to motivate a physics-inspired model of gaze mapped from neural activity \cite{doi:10.1126/science.1166112, CARPENTER2000R291, Sparks2002-zb}. The self-avoidance condition is mechanistically very similar to the kernel adaptation, which we introduce in this thesis to explain rhythmicity in saccades. This work successfully reproduces superdiffusion in fixations and the presence of microsaccades. This model, however, accounts only for fixational motion, not for the long saccades that organise it. Furthermore, no tremor-frequency peak is observed.

DeepGaze~III \cite{kummerer_deepgaze_2022} models fixation prediction through a state-of-the-art deep convolutional neural network, a map of observed oculomotor biases (such as an observed centre-bias in viewing an image), and a history of previous fixations. This can predict the locations with the highest accuracy to date across all metrics in the standard MIT/Tübingen Saliency Benchmark \cite{mit-tuebingen-saliency-benchmark, kummerer_deepgaze_2022}. This model is highly successful at predicting fixation and has been used to study patterns in attention by simulating saccade prediction without requiring human measurements; however, it lacks biological or functional utility beyond fixation position. The model provides no temporal information, only providing the location of fixations, and does not consider the dynamics of fixations themselves, nor does it provide any mapping from biologically realistic neural activity onto the dynamics of gaze.

In developing a physics-inspired model of gaze dynamics, the continuous-time dynamics and spatiotemporal patterns of gaze are of great interest. But each of these models omits the spatiotemporal patterns in saccade dynamics and the high-frequency timescale of tremor during fixation. This thesis links well-studied neural timescales to gaze dynamics. It is then valuable to outline the functional interpretations of such frequency bands.

\subsection{Timescales of neural computation}\label{subsec:neur_freq_bands}
Neural firing activity is divided into frequency bands named by Greek letters, originally discovered and decomposed from electroencephalogram (EEG) data \cite{fan_relation_2007, klimesch_frequency_2018}. Divisions are only approximate, but bands may be thus considered: delta (\SIrange{0.1}{3}{\hertz}), theta (\SIrange{4}{8}{\hertz}), alpha (\SIrange{8}{14}{\hertz}), beta (\SIrange{14}{40}{\hertz}), and gamma (\SIrange{40}{100}{\hertz}) \cite{fan_relation_2007, klimesch_frequency_2018}. Of particular interest to this thesis are the interpretations of theta and gamma neural activity as functionally important to neural computation. Broadly, theta rhythms have been found to modulate and organise bursts in gamma-scale neural activity  \cite{qi_fractional_2022, fries_mechanism_2005, fries_rhythmic_2023, fries_rhythms_2015, spyropoulos_theta_2018, bastos_communication_2015}. Theta rhythms have been shown to modulate attention during auditory tasks \cite{wang_attention_2022, yang_neural_2024}, visual search tasks \cite{kawashima_attentional_2022, spyropoulos_theta_2018, Mo_competing_2019}, and when analysing the broad attentional mechanism of the brain \cite{tan_theta_2024, fan_relation_2007, klimesch_frequency_2018}. 

Theta-rhythmic cycles are receiving strong support in the cognitive neuroscience literature as organising and communicating information sampled from the environment for integration into the brain's internal model \cite{fries_mechanism_2005, fries_rhythmic_2023, fries_rhythms_2015, spyropoulos_theta_2018, bastos_communication_2015}. Strong direct evidence for this theory includes correlations between intelligence and high theta peak power in EEG data during tasks requiring focus and attention \cite{wang_attention_2022, tan_theta_2024}, and the discovered sensitivity of the visual system to react and search scenes on a theta rhythm \cite{kawashima_attentional_2022, spyropoulos_theta_2018}. Accordingly, the firing patterns which define the lowest level of computation carried out in the brain are regularly found to be gamma in timescale, with broadband signals being found within \SIrange{40}{100}{\hertz} in a wide range of measurements of neural activity during cognitive tasks \cite{canolty_high_2006, harris2026-cn, ursino_thetagamma_2024, brooks_thetagamma_2020, tort_thetagamma_2009}. The coupling between the phase of theta activity and the amplitude of gamma activity is known as theta-gamma coupling (TGC). This phenomenon, a specific form of phase-amplitude coupling (PAC), is schematically represented in \cref{fig:litrev-thetagamma_coupling}. The evidence and theory behind TGC are described in a systematic review by \textcite{ursino_thetagamma_2024}. This thesis provides a computational view of the function of TGC within the search dynamics of gaze, as modulating periods of fixational attention on a slow theta scale, during which information is dynamically sampled on a gamma scale.
