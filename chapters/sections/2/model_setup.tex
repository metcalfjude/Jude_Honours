\section{Model setup}\label{sec:model_setup}
\subsubsection*{Mathematical formulation of gaze dynamics}
The dynamical model of gaze dynamics, inspired by and developed from the FNS theory of neural dynamics (see \cref{subsec:neur_sampling}, \cite{qi_fractional_2022}), models eye movement trajectories as an overdamped Langevin system driven by an adaptive potential landscape $V_t(\cdot)$ by a kernel of information gathering $k(\cdot)$. Let $\mathbf{x}_t$ denote the two-dimensional position of visual attention, $\mathbf{p}_t$ a generalised auxiliary variable analogous to momentum and encodes short-scale memory effects, $\beta \geq 0$ the coupling strength parameter between $\mathbf{p}_t$ and $\mathbf{x}_t$, $\gamma > 0$ the noise strength parameter as in the standard Langevin system \ref{eq:langevin}, and $0 < \alpha \le 2$ the Lévy tail exponent which defines the heaviness of tails from which noise is drawn, and equivalently the strength of superdiffusion as in \crefrange{subsec:anom_diff}{subsec:levy_motion}. The driving noise process $dL_t^{\alpha}$ represents independent symmetric $\alpha$-stable ($\mathcal{S}\alpha\mathcal{S}$) Lévy fluctuations.

A deterministic drift $b_\alpha(\mathbf{x}_t)$ drives the system driven by Lévy noise toward an invariant target distribution $\pi(\mathbf{x})$ over long times. As derived in \cite{qi_fractional_2022}, the exact non-local drift for arbitrary $0 < \alpha < 2$ is formulated in terms of the Riesz $(\alpha - 2)$--fractional derivative $\mathcal{D}^{\alpha - 2}_{\mathbf{x}}$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:full_drift}
	b_{\alpha}(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{\mathcal{D}^{\alpha - 2}_{\mathbf{x}}[\pi(\mathbf{x}) \nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \ln\pi(\mathbf{x})]}{\pi(\mathbf{x})}.
\end{equation}
where these nonlocal fractional dynamics are treated in detail in \cref{sec:levy_superdiffusion}. However, due to the computationally intense calculation of the nonlocal operator $\mathcal{D}^{\alpha - 2}$, in this work we adopt the local gradient approximation of $\mathcal{D}_{\textbf{x}}^{\alpha-2}$, which becomes exact as $\alpha \rightarrow 2$, as given in Equation~(14) of \cite{simsekli_fractional_nodate}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:drift_approx}
	b_{\alpha}(\mathbf{x}) \approx c_{\alpha} \nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \ln \pi(\mathbf{x}), \qquad c_{\alpha} = \frac{\Gamma(\alpha - 1)}{\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{2})^2}.
\end{equation}
Combining these components, the full coupled system for gaze dynamics evaluated throughout this work is given by:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fns_system}
	\begin{aligned}
		d\mathbf{x}_t &= -\gamma c_{\alpha} \nabla V_{t}(\mathbf{x})dt + \beta \mathbf{p}_t dt + \gamma^{1/\alpha} dL_t^{\alpha},\\
		d\mathbf{p}_t &= - \beta c_{\alpha} \nabla V_{t}(\mathbf{x}) dt,\\
		\partial_t V_{t}(\mathbf{x}) &= \frac{1}{\tau}k(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_t), 
		\qquad V_0(\mathbf{x}) = S(\mathbf{x})
	\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where as $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ are parameters modulating FNS dynamics, $c_{\alpha}$ corrects the scale of the gradient drift, and $\tau$ controls information updates to the dynamical-informative potential $V_t(\textbf{x})$. The nature and purpose of the dynamical updates $k(\cdot)$ is described in \cref{sec:dynamical-information_adaptation}. Importantly, due to the equivalent relationship of a Langevin potential in to its targeted distribution in \cref{eq:boltzmann-gibbs} and the definition of saliency in visual sampling as in \cref{eq:saliency}, we can construct a continuous-time updating internal-state belief distribution of the search target:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pi_t_V_t_equation}
	\pi_t(\textbf{x}) \propto \exp \left[ -V_t(\textbf{x}) \right]
\end{equation}
\subsubsection*{Empirical protocol}
To validate this modelled computational framework against human behaviour, we emulate the visual foraging task designed by \textcite{naher_primate_saccades}, which requires subjects to sequentially scan an array of dots to locate a hidden target. This is done by choosing biologically accurate values for parameters $\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\tau$ and the information gathering scale of $k(\cdot)$. The task itself is encoded in the initial saliency map $V_0(\textbf{x}) = \log \pi_0(\textbf{x})$. These parameters and choice of $\pi_0(\textbf{x})$ are described in the final part of this section.

In the human-testing protocol examined by \textcite{naher_primate_saccades}, trials commenced with a pre-search fixation period of duration drawn uniformly between \SIrange{2500}{3500}{\milli \second}. Following a \qty{100}{\milli \second} duration of no stimulus, participants were presented with a search array of white dots, each individual dot subtending \qty{0.3}{\degree} of visual angle on the screen. Exactly 20\% of these dots functioned as true targets, which were visually identical to non-targets, and subjects were allotted a maximum search time of 5000~ms to successfully foveate a target, at which point an audio cue would indicate a success, and the subject would be required to fixate for another post-fixation period of \SIrange{2500}{3500}{\milli \second}. To investigate the influence of spatial structure, the study compared a REGULAR task---where dots were spaced identically to the centers of an invisible hexagonal tiling---with an IRREGULAR task, which randomly redistributed the same number of dots across the screen to break spatial regularity. Assuming a standard viewing distance of \qty{57.3}{\cm} (where $1^{\circ} \approx 1~\text{cm}$) and a monitor pixel density of \qty{36.22}{\px\per\cm}, spatial measurements translate directly between pixels and degrees using a scaling factor of \qty{36.22}{\px\per\degree}.

\subsubsection*{Computational model \& standard parameter setup}
The empirical conditions from \textcite{naher_primate_saccades} are translated into numerical model parameters using spatial units of pixels ($\unit{\px}$) and temporal units of seconds ($\unit{\second}$). The baseline stimulus potential $S(\mathbf{x})$ is constructed on a 2D grid of Gaussian distributions representing quadratic potential wells over a $16 \times 16$ square grid (256 wells total), with 20\% randomly designated as targets. Based on fixational eye movement distributions, the standard deviation of each well is set to $\sigma_{\text{well}} = 3~\unit{\px}$. The average axial distance between wells is fixed at \qty{110}{\px} ($\approx \qty{3.04}{\degree}$ visual angle), bridging the dense (\qty{2.3}{\degree}) and intermediate (\qty{4.5}{\degree}) spacings in the REGULAR human search condition.

The baseline parameters for standard simulation runs are calibrated as follows:
\begin{itemize}
	\item $\alpha = 1.2$: Captures the characteristic search superdiffusion observed in \cref{sec:search_superdiffusion} while maintaining numerical stability.
	\item $\sigma_{k} = 80~\unit{\px}$: Defines an adaptive spatial acuity kernel by the Gaussian $k(\mathbf{x}) = \exp \left( -\frac{\|\mathbf{x}\|^2}{2\sigma_{k}^2} \right)$. This matches the radial decay scale of human visual acuity ($\approx \qty{2.5}{\degree}$, corresponding to 87.2~\unit{\px} at a \qty{50}{\cm} viewing distance with 4~\unit{\px\per\mm} density).
	\item $\tau = 2 \times 10^{-4}~\unit{\second}$: Set to yield theta-rhythmic saccadic transitions, as derived in \cref{eq:theta_rhythm} and evaluated in \cref{sec:theta_rhythmicity_of_saccades}. (Note: across simulations, this produces an average ISI of 360~ms, mirroring the empirical saccade rate of 3.53~Hz / 280~ms average ISI).
	\item $\beta = 1000~\unit{\px~\second^{-1}}$ and $\gamma = 1000~\unit{\px^{\alpha}~\second^{-1}}$: Ensures recovery of gamma-rhythmic harmonic oscillations within the underdamped regime for \qty{3}{\px} wells (\cref{sec:gamma_oscillations}).
\end{itemize}
