\section{Model setup}\label{sec:model_setup}

Having established the individual spatiotemporal signatures of FNS dynamics, we now define a standard computational framework to test these dynamics against the human behavioral data presented in \cref{chap:empirical_validations}. 

\subsubsection*{Mathematical formulation of gaze dynamics}
The dynamical model of gaze dynamics, inspired by and developed from the FNS theory of neural dynamics, models eye movement trajectories as an underdamped Langevin system driven by an adaptive potential landscape $V_t(\textbf{x})$ by a kernel of information gathering $k(\cdot)$. Let $\mathbf{x}_t$ denote the two-dimensional position vector, $\mathbf{p}_t$ a generalised auxiliary variable analogous to momentum, $\beta > 0$ the coupling strength parameter between $\mathbf{p}_t$ and $\mathbf{x}_t$, $\gamma > 0$ the noise strength parameter, and $0 < \alpha \le 2$ the Lévy tail exponent. The driving noise process $dL_t^{\alpha}$ represents independent symmetric $\alpha$-stable ($\mathcal{S}\alpha\mathcal{S}$) Lévy fluctuations.

The deterministic drift $b_\alpha(\mathbf{x}_t)$ drives the system toward an invariant target distribution $\pi(\mathbf{x})$. As derived in \cite{qi_fractional_2022}, the exact non-local drift for arbitrary $0 < \alpha < 2$ is formulated in terms of the $(\alpha - 2)$--order Riesz fractional derivative $\mathcal{D}^{\alpha - 2}_{\mathbf{x}}$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:full_drift}
	b_{\alpha}(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{\mathcal{D}^{\alpha - 2}_{\mathbf{x}}[\pi(\mathbf{x}) \nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \ln\pi(\mathbf{x})]}{\pi(\mathbf{x})}.
\end{equation}
where these nonlocal fractional dynamics are treated in detail in \cref{sec:levy_superdiffusion}. However, in this work we adopt the local gradient approximation, which becomes exact as $\alpha \rightarrow 2$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:drift_approx}
	b_{\alpha}(\mathbf{x}) \approx c_{\alpha} \nabla_{\mathbf{x}} \ln \pi(\mathbf{x}), \qquad c_{\alpha} = \frac{\Gamma(\alpha - 1)}{\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{2})^2}.
\end{equation}
Combining these components, the full coupled system for gaze dynamics evaluated throughout this work is given by:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fns_system}
	\begin{aligned}
		d\mathbf{x}_t &= -\gamma c_{\alpha} \nabla V_{t}(\mathbf{x})dt + \beta \mathbf{p}_t dt + \gamma^{1/\alpha} dL_t^{\alpha},\\
		d\mathbf{p}_t &= - \beta c_{\alpha} \nabla V_{t}(\mathbf{x}) dt,\\
		\partial_t V_{t}(\mathbf{x}) &= \frac{1}{\tau}k(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_t), 
		\qquad V_0(\mathbf{x}) = S(\mathbf{x})
	\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where as $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ are parameters modulating FNS dynamics, $c_{\alpha}$ corrects the scale of the gradient drift, and $\tau$ controls information updates to the dynamical-informative potential $V_t(\textbf{x})$. The nature and purpose of the dynamical updates $k(\cdot)$ is described in \cref{sec:dynamical-information_adaptation}. Importantly, due to the relationship of a Langevin potential to a targeted distribution as in \cref{eq:saliency}, we can construct a continuous-time updating internal-state belief distribution of the search target:
\begin{equation}
	\pi_t(\textbf{x}) \propto \exp \left[ -V_t(\textbf{x}) \right]
\end{equation}
\subsubsection*{Empirical protocol from Näher et al.}
To validate this modelled computational framework against human behaviour, we emulate the visual foraging task designed by \textcite{naher_primate_saccades}, which requires subjects to sequentially scan an array of dots to locate a hidden target. This is done by choosing biologically accurate values for parameters $\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\tau$ and the information gathering scale of $k(\cdot)$. The task itself is encoded in the initial saliency map $V_0(\textbf{x}) = \log \pi_0(\textbf{x})$. These parameters and choice of $\pi_0(\textbf{x})$ are described in the final part of this section.

In the human protocol established by \textcite{naher_primate_saccades}, trials commenced with a pre-search fixation period of 2500 to 3500~ms. Following a 100~ms gap, participants were presented with a search array of white dots, each subtending \qty{0.3}{\degree} of visual angle. Exactly 20\% of these dots functioned as true targets, and subjects were allotted a maximum search time of 5000~ms to successfully foveate a target. To investigate the influence of spatial structure, the study compared a REGULAR task---where dots were spaced identically to the centers of an invisible hexagonal tiling---with an IRREGULAR task, which randomly redistributed the same number of dots across the screen to break spatial regularity. Assuming a standard viewing distance of \qty{57.3}{\cm} (where $1^{\circ} \approx 1~\text{cm}$) and a monitor pixel density of \qty{36.22}{\px\per\cm}, spatial measurements translate directly between pixels and degrees using a scaling factor of \qty{36.22}{\px\per\degree}.

\subsubsection*{Computational model setup \& parameter calibration}
The empirical conditions from \textcite{naher_primate_saccades} are translated into numerical model parameters using spatial units of pixels ($\unit{\px}$) and temporal units of seconds ($\unit{\second}$). The baseline stimulus potential $S(\mathbf{x})$ is constructed on a 2D grid of Gaussian distributions representing quadratic potential wells over a $16 \times 16$ square grid (256 wells total), with 20\% randomly designated as targets. Based on fixational eye movement distributions, the standard deviation of each well is set to $\sigma_{\text{well}} = 3~\unit{\px}$. The average axial distance between wells is fixed at \qty{110}{\px} ($\approx \qty{3.04}{\degree}$ visual angle), bridging the dense (\qty{2.3}{\degree}) and intermediate (\qty{4.5}{\degree}) spacings in the REGULAR human search condition.

The baseline parameters for standard simulation runs are calibrated as follows:
\begin{itemize}
	\item $\alpha = 1.2$: Captures the characteristic search superdiffusion observed in \cref{sec:search_superdiffusion} while maintaining numerical stability.
	\item $\sigma_{k} = 80~\unit{\px}$: Defines an adaptive spatial acuity kernel by the Gaussian $k(\mathbf{x}) = \exp \left( -\frac{\|\mathbf{x}\|^2}{2\sigma_{k}^2} \right)$. This matches the radial decay scale of human visual acuity ($\approx \qty{2.5}{\degree}$, corresponding to 87.2~\unit{\px} at a \qty{50}{\cm} viewing distance with 4~\unit{\px\per\mm} density).
	\item $\tau = 2 \times 10^{-4}~\unit{\second^{-1}}$: Set to yield theta-rhythmic saccadic transitions, as derived in \cref{eq:theta_rhythm} and evaluated in \cref{sec:theta_rhythmicity_of_saccades}. (Note: across simulations, this produces an average ISI of 360~ms, mirroring the empirical saccade rate of 3.53~Hz / 280~ms average ISI).
	\item $\beta = 1000~\unit{\px~\second^{-1}}$ and $\gamma = 1000~\unit{\px^2~\second^{-1}}$: Ensures recovery of gamma-rhythmic harmonic oscillations within the underdamped regime for \qty{3}{\px} wells (\cref{sec:gamma_oscillations}).
\end{itemize}
