\section{Dynamical-information adaptation}\label{sec:dynamical-information_adaptation}
The component of our gaze model that connects dynamics to information is the dynamic-informative kernel. It is the principal extension of FNS work towards a full computational model of visual search during active vision \cite{qi_fractional_2022, chen_spatiotemporal_2022}. We call the kernel dynamical-informative because it couples two processes: the dynamics of the FNS sampler as it explores a landscape, and the information gained during that exploration when interpreted as active search. Its development, therefore, draws on two bodies of literature. The first is statistical physics and computational sampling, where an analogous process of kernel deposition is called metadynamics (see \textcite{barducci_well-tempered_2008} for its introduction and \textcite{bussi_using_2020} for the ``well-tempered'' variant, which balances sampling accuracy with fast-mixing ergodicity). The second is the psychophysics of how spatial acuity limits information gathering during search, and how information informs the dynamics of search.

The section proceeds as follows: \Cref{subsec:motivations} states the problems and motivations that inform the addition of this kernel. \Cref{subsec:information_as_potential} derives its form from Bayesian information gathering. We then examine the spatial scale of this information gathering by considering human visual acuity. \Cref{subsec:adaptive_kernel_dynamics} analyses the resulting dynamics and shows that they encode the theta-rhythmic timescale of saccades studied in \cref{sec:theta_rhythmicity_of_saccades}, as well as the rate of information gain explored throughout \cref{chap:functional_advantages}. 
\subsection{Motivations for kernel adaptation}\label{subsec:motivations}
The motivations for the addition of a dynamic landscape adaptation of the model are threefold. Firstly, the quantity of information gathered by the gaze trajectory must be accounted for, as this is the fundamental purpose of a visual search. Secondly, the regularity of information-seeking saccades during visual search must be present, most importantly the recently discovered phenomena of strongly theta-rhythmic saccades explored further in \cref{sec:theta_rhythmicity_of_saccades}, as well as the well-studied phenomenon of inhibition of return (IoR) \cite{ludwig_mechanism_2009, redden_inhibition_2021}. Thirdly, any functionally informative description of search dynamics must couple these search dynamics to the current information to model search through active vision \cite{Renninger2005-ee, Bruce_2009_Saliency, mcdonnell_introductory_2011, kittler_effective_2026}. 

To couple gaze dynamics with continuously acquired information, we examine the structure of visual information gathering. In this work, the target distribution $\pi_t(\textbf{x})$ will encode the belief in the presence of a searched-for target object in 2D space, in a Bayesian sense. Thus, any information during exploration of non-target locations will cause a depletion of belief (probability mass) from the target distribution $\pi_t$ locally. This is equivalent in the dynamical model to raising the potential landscape $V_t(\textbf{x}) = -\ln\pi_t(\textbf{x})$ locally by some kernel centred at the gaze position whose width represents the spatial acuity of information gathering. This gives a well-motivated definition of continuous information gathering, as information is directly equated with the logarithm of the probability of reducing uncertainty, and the uncertainty in the target's location is exactly $\pi_t(\textbf{x})$ in our search task. 

In this section, we show that the following requirements are satisfied by the novel addition of a single mechanism to FNS dynamics to construct a full model of gaze dynamics:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Updating search information.} The quantity of information gathered along the gaze trajectory must be tracked internally, as this is the fundamental purpose of a visual search.
    \item \emph{Saccadic regularities.} The model must reproduce the regularities in information-seeking saccades: most importantly, by the recently discovered strongly theta-rhythmicity of saccades (\cref{sec:theta_rhythmicity_of_saccades}), and the well-studied phenomenon of inhibition of return (IoR) \cite{ludwig_mechanism_2009, redden_inhibition_2021}.
    \item \emph{Coupling dynamics and searched information.} Any functionally informative description of search dynamics must couple those dynamics to the information gathered so far, completing the feedback loop of active vision \cite{Renninger2005-ee, Bruce_2009_Saliency, mcdonnell_introductory_2011, kittler_effective_2026}.
\end{enumerate}


\subsection{Developing the model kernel adaptation as information gathering}\label{subsec:information_as_potential}
In this work, the target distribution $\pi_t(\mathbf{x}) = e^{-V_t(\mathbf{x})}$ encodes, in a Bayesian sense, the belief that the searched-for target lies at position $\mathbf{x}$. Fixating a location $\mathbf{x}_0$ without finding the target is evidence against the target lying near $\mathbf{x}_0$, so considering some limited and spatially local information gathering during a fixation, belief must be depleted according to some spatial kernel function $K(\cdot)$ as:
\begin{equation*}
    \pi_{t+\mathrm{d}t}(\mathbf{x}) \propto \pi_t(\mathbf{x})\, e^{-K(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_0) dt},
\end{equation*}
where $K(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_0) \geq 0$ is the log-evidence per unit time against discovering a target at $\mathbf{x}$ gathered during $\mathrm{d}t$, which is most informative at $\mathbf{x}_0$ where the most information may be gathered (see \cite{bujia_modeling_2022, Ruarte2024.11.29.626088}). The dynamics of $\mathbf{x}_t$ are then driven by the effective potential $V_t(\mathbf{x}) = -\ln \pi_t(\mathbf{x})$, so this multiplicative depletion of belief is an additive increment to the potential, $V_{t+dt}(\mathbf{x}) = V_{t}(\mathbf{x}) + K(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_0)dt$, up to a spatially uniform constant which normalises the distribution $\pi_t$ and acts as a scalar gauge symmetry on the potential $V_t$. 

Information gathering is therefore equivalent to raising the potential locally by the information gained at each location. This gives a well-motivated, continuous definition of information gathering, which turns out to be identical to an additive modulation of the potential surface which controls the dynamics due to their shared link to an internal belief distribution $\pi_t$. The amount gathered at $\mathbf{x}$ depends on its displacement from the gaze position, because acuity falls off away from the fovea. Writing $K(\mathbf{x}) \mathrm{d}t = k(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_0)\,\mathrm{d}t/\tau$ to modulate the timescale of information gathering using $\tau$, and to use a dimensionless kernel function $k(\cdot)$, the instantaneous law of kernel dynamics for gaze at $\mathbf{x}_t$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel_law}
    \partial_t V_{t}(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{\tau}\,k(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_t),
\end{equation}
where $k(\cdot)$ is an approximately radially symmetric function that tends to zero for large arguments and is scaled so that $k(\mathbf{0})=1$. Throughout this work we use an isotropic Gaussian,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaussian_kernel}
    k(\mathbf{x}) = \exp\left( -\frac{\|\mathbf{x}\|^2}{2\sigma_k^2} \right).
\end{equation}
the two parameters $\sigma_k$ and $\tau$ control the spatial and temporal kernel dynamics respectively. The time constant $\tau$ is the time a gaze position needs to raise the potential by one nat at its centre, so $1/\tau$ is the peak rate of information acquisition. The width $\sigma_k$ is the spatial scale of information gathering. We will see that $\tau$ is related to theta-rhythmic activity in gaze dynamics (\cref{sec:theta_rhythmicity_of_saccades}). We can now show that $\sigma_k$ is not a free parameter of our model but is set by the acuity of the human eye.

\subsubsection*{Relation of the kernel width to spatial acuity}
The width $\sigma_k$ is tied to how the information gathering accuracy of a target falls with retinal eccentricity $E$, the angular distance from the point of gaze. This is quantified by the visibility map $d'(E)$, measured for a given target-background pair. Ideal-searcher models \cite{Ruarte2024.11.29.626088, najemnik_optimal_2005, zhou_human_2021, Renninger2005-ee, Bruce_2009_Saliency} use such acuity scales to weight the evidence from each fixation in the Bayesian update of target belief \cite{najemnik_optimal_2005, najemnik_eye_2008}. The visibility map declines with eccentricity because of the decreased resolution outside the central fovea \cite{ZHAOPING2026103211, kelly1962information, sziklai1956some}, through the fall-off of acuity, cortical magnification and crowding, with a half-resolution eccentricity $E_2$ of a few degrees (about $2.3^{\circ}$ in \textcite{geisler_real-time_1998}) \cite{levi_vernier_1985, strasburger_peripheral_2011}. For an observer with sensitivity $d'$, the expected log-evidence against a target at a target-absent location is $d'^2/2$ per fixation \cite{najemnik_optimal_2005, najemnik_eye_2008}, so the kernel is the normalised squared visibility map,
\begin{equation*}
    k(E) = \left[\frac{d'(E)}{d'(0)}\right]^{2},
\end{equation*}
and $\sigma_k$ is the width of this profile. Thus $\sigma_k$ is determined by the acuity scaling of the eye and the spatial scale of the target rather than being a free parameter. Calculating the $E_2$ of our model as the characteristic scale of our Gaussian $\sigma_k$ as defined in \cref{sec:model_setup}, we find a $E_2 = \sigma_k \div \qty{36.22}{\px \degree^{-1}} = (80 \div 36.22)\unit{\degree} \approx \qty{2.20}{\degree}$, which is biologically accurate for such a search task as we consider.

\subsection{Adaptive kernel dynamics as a driver of long-time rhythmic exploration}\label{subsec:adaptive_kernel_dynamics}
We now predict analytically the rate of excursions under these modelled dynamics in \cref{eq:fns_system} due to kernel deposition, whose mechanism is schematically shown in \cref{fig:kernel_theta_panel}\textbf{a)}--\textbf{d)}. The central idea for understanding these kernel-driven transitions is to formulate a tractable transition condition: the effective potential well becomes concave down at the gaze position. Such a well no longer localises the gaze position $\textbf{x}_t$, and the Lévy-driven noise will then produce excursions to other modes.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/kernel_four_panel.pdf}
	\caption[width=\linewidth]{\textbf{Schematic representation of kernel dynamics influencing an information-driven transition between potential wells.} \textbf{a)} Two potential wells in a 1D potential landscape, with a high potential region separating them, which would represent low saliency in a model of gaze. $\textbf{x}_0$ is positioned in the left well, which represents gaze position. \textbf{b)} Information acquisition as the kernel (orange) grows, gaining information and raising the potential landscape locally. \textbf{c)} A saccade-like transition, where the left potential well is inverted, followed by a transition to the right potential well. \textbf{d)} Exploration in the right well, with the information-gathering cycle resetting on a long $t_{\theta}$-scale.}
	\label{fig:kernel_theta_panel}
\end{figure}
We consider a single isotropic Gaussian mode of the target distribution, centred at zero with standard deviation $\sigma_{\text{fix}}$, and gaze position fixating within it. In terms of the radial coordinate $r$ about the mode, the effective potential $V_t(r)$ therefore starts as the quadratic well
\begin{equation*}
    V_0(r) = -\ln\pi_0(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{r^2}{2\sigma_{\text{fix}}^2}.
\end{equation*}
The choice of Gaussian loses very little generality, because a Taylor expansion will show that locally every mode is approximated to second order by a quadratic well---referred to as Laplace's approximation. We assume that the gaze position is localised near $r\approx 0$ and that $\sigma_k \gg \sigma_{\text{fix}}$, so that the small noise-driven motion about the tight potential well with width $\sigma_{\text{fix}}$ can be smoothed over, and kernel deposition can be assumed to be centred at $r=0$. Excursions are then driven solely by the deterministic kernel dynamics. Large Lévy jumps will introduce Poisson-distributed transitions, as a result of the Lévy-Itô decomposition discussed in \cref{subsec:levy_motion} \cite{Kyprianou2014}, which will be arrhythmic with some mean value. We do not analyse this addition to rhythmic transitions between wells, but we discover this in the data in \cref{chap:empirical_validations}, and it is shown for several kernel rates $\tau$ in \cref{tab:theta_comparison}. Under \cref{eq:kernel_law} with the gaze at the mode centre,
\begin{equation*}
    \partial_t V_t(r) = \frac{1}{\tau}\exp\left(-\frac{r^2}{2\sigma_k^2}\right),
\end{equation*}
which integrates to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:effective_potential}
    V_t(r) = \frac{r^2}{2\sigma_{\text{fix}}^2} + \frac{t}{\tau} \exp \left(-\frac{r^2}{2\sigma_k^2}\right).
\end{equation}

The excursion criterion here is a negative radial second derivative of the explored potential at the fixation point $r=0$, leaving the gaze unconstrained. The second spatial derivative is
\begin{equation*}
    \frac{\partial^2 V_t(r)}{\partial r^2} = \frac{1}{\sigma_{\text{fix}}^2} + \frac{t}{\tau\,\sigma_k^2}\left(\frac{r^2}{\sigma_k^2} - 1\right)\exp\left\{-\frac{r^2}{2\sigma_k^2}\right\},
\end{equation*}
and at $r=0$ (where the Hessian is isotropic), it vanishes at the time
\begin{equation}\label{eq:theta_rhythm}
    t_{\theta} = \tau\,\frac{\sigma_k^2}{\sigma_{\text{fix}}^2}.
\end{equation}
For $t>t_\theta$, the origin is a local maximum of $V_t$, making this explored region of low interest and unlikely to be explored, which yields a dynamical origin of inhibition of return \cite{redden_inhibition_2021, van_ede_looking_2026, kingstone_inhibition_1999, klein_visual_2023, henderson_oculomotor_2012, ludwig_mechanism_2009}: the gaze is expelled from the location it has just exploited. Strictly, the criterion marks the onset of delocalisation; the Lévy noise then carries the excursion to another mode.

Unsurprisingly, $t_\theta$ is proportional to $\tau$. The dependence on the ratio of variances is of more interest. The dwell time is proportional to the variance $\sigma_k^2$ of the adaptive kernel and inversely proportional to the variance $\sigma_{\text{fix}}^2$ of the mode: a broad kernel takes longer to flatten a given well, and a sharply distributed fixation point will confine the gaze for longer. The result also extends beyond the Gaussian kernel, as $\sigma_k$ represents the curvature of the kernel function at zero $k(\mathbf{0})$. An extension of the kernel method to functions that have a cusp at the origin may be biologically realistic \cite{geisler_real-time_1998, levi_vernier_1985, strasburger_peripheral_2011}, and could produce a rich description of the drifts and fixational movement of the eyes beyond tremor frequencies \cite{wu_fixational_2024, rucci_control_2015}. Still, such an approach is less analytically tractable and less computationally stable.
