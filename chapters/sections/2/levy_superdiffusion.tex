\section{Lévy superdiffusion}\label{sec:levy_superdiffusion}
To model the superdiffusive dynamics associated with exploratory probabilistic dynamics during visual search, we drive our system with Lévy noise in an overdamped Langevin regime (see \crefrange{subsec:brownian_motion}{subsec:levy_motion}). Lévy flights provide a canonical example of superdiffusive motion (see \cref{subsec:levy_motion}). In our model, however, the adaptive potential landscape and auxiliary momentum dynamics introduce additional complexity to this behaviour. Quantifying superdiffusion in this system, exploring a confined and dynamic potential landscape, requires a dedicated analysis of how Lévy dynamics will introduce monoscaling superdiffusion, which is self-similar on all scales, with the same superdiffusive patterns driving large jumps across a landscape as those small-scale jumps when considering finer movements. Lévy motion unifies these scales under a single superdiffusive pattern, motivating our unification of gaze dynamics under a single model.

\subsubsection*{Model dynamics as a generalised Lévy-driven Langevin system}
With the aim of understanding the superdiffusive scaling of gaze, we look to our model as a generalised form of the Lévy processes, the canonical monoscaling process. Lévy flight superdiffusion is analysed from the deterministic dynamics of the probability distribution of jump size $P(\| \Delta \textbf{x} \|)$. This problem of understanding the dynamics of the distribution of $\textbf{x}$ is identical to that encountered when studying classical Brownian motion, for which the classical Fokker-Planck equation was developed (see \ref{subsec:brownian_motion}). Here, in the case of a generalised Lévy-driven Langevin system with momentum coupling, the deterministic dynamics of the distribution of $\textbf{x}$ is governed by the fractional Fokker-Planck equation, as described in \cite{barkai_fractional_2001, qi_fractional_2022, ijcai2018p419}. We can consider the phase space of a generalised coupled set of $n$ stochastic differential equations of $d$-dimensions of $\mathbf{z}=[\mathbf{z}_1, \mathbf{z}_2, \cdots, \mathbf{z}_n]^T$, where $d\mathbf{z}_t = (\mathbf{D} + \mathbf{Q}) \, b(\mathbf{z}_t)dt + \mathbf{D}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}} d\mathbf{L}^{\alpha}_t$ for $(n \times d)$--dimensional Lévy noise. Here, $\mathbf{D}$ is the positive semi-definite matrix which defines the strength and coupling of noise across $\mathbf{z}$, and $\mathbf{Q}$ represents the skew-symmetric matrix defining the dynamical coupling across $\mathbf{z}$. For such a system, the fractional Fokker-Planck equation can be expressed exactly:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fractional_fokker_planck}
    \partial_t P(\mathbf{z}, t) = - (\mathbf{D} + \textbf{Q}) \nabla_\mathbf{z} \left[ b(\mathbf{z}) P(\mathbf{z}, t)\right] - \mathbf{D} \mathcal{D}_{\mathbf{z}}^{\alpha} P(\mathbf{z}, t)
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}_{\mathbf{z}}$ represents the $\alpha$-fractional Riesz derivative, which generalises the operation of the Laplacian to fractional orders, and is defined in Fourier space as $\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}_{\mathbf{z}}[f] = \mathcal{F}^{-1} \|\textbf{k}\|^{\alpha} \mathcal{F}[f]$, with the case of $\alpha = 2$ restoring the ordinary Fokker-Planck equation with $\mathcal{D}^{\alpha = 2}_{\mathbf{z}} = -\nabla_{\mathbf{z}}^2$. It can be shown that our model of gaze dynamics is recovered for $\mathbf{z} = [\textbf{x}, \textbf{p}]$, $\mathbf{D} = \begin{bmatrix}
\gamma & 0\\
0 & 0
\end{bmatrix}$ 
and $\mathbf{Q} = \begin{bmatrix}
0 & -\beta\\
\beta & 0
\end{bmatrix}$, shown in \cite{qi_fractional_2022}. The $b(\mathbf{z})$ drift term required to recover a given distribution is presented in \crefrange{eq:full_drift}{eq:drift_approx}. Any solution to the dynamics in \cref{eq:fractional_fokker_planck} is nontrivial, as the fractional derivative yields a nonlinear multivariate integro-differential equation that is difficult to propagate, even numerically. 

\subsubsection*{Analysis of Lévy flight superdiffusion from the fractional Fokker-Planck equation}
When considering diffusion over short timescales, the physics of fractional Langevin systems with $\alpha > 1$ are noise-dominated. In our Lévy-driven system, short times are dominated by $\alpha$-stable $dL^{\alpha}_t$ Lévy noise, with power-law-distributed spatial scales of short jumps and nonlocal jumps to distant positions in short times. The scaling of Lévy noise corroborates this as $dL^{\alpha}_{\Delta t} = \Delta t^{1/\alpha} dL^{\alpha}_1$ in \cref{eq:levy_noise} is stronger than ballistic effects, which scale in short times as $\Delta t$. Thus, considering the fractional Fokker-Planck equation evolving from a delta function initial condition $P(\mathbf{z},0) = \delta^n(\textbf{x} -\textbf{x}_0)\delta^n(\textbf{p} - \textbf{p}_0)$ evolving through a quadratic potential using the position coordinate $\Delta \textbf{x} = \textbf{x} - \textbf{x}_0$, yielding a drift term $\textbf{b}(\Delta \textbf{x}) = \textbf{b}(\textbf{x}_0) + \mathcal{O}(\Delta \textbf{x})$. We can construct a leading-order short-time solution up to $t=\Delta t$ and marginalise over the auxiliary momentum $\textbf{p}$, which in practice is a nuisance variable, we have:
\begin{equation}
    \partial_t P(\Delta \textbf{x},t) = -\gamma\mathcal{D}_{\textbf{x}}^{\alpha}P(\Delta \textbf{x}, \Delta t) - \nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\cdot
    \left[\left(\gamma \mathbf{b}(\Delta \textbf{x}) + \beta\,\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{p}\,|\,\Delta \textbf{x},\Delta t]\right)P(\Delta \textbf{x},\Delta t)\right]
\end{equation}
where we can truncate these terms to the highest order using
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{b}(\Delta \textbf{x}) = \mathbf{b}_0 + \mathcal{O}(\Delta \textbf{x}),\qquad 
    \mathbb{E}[\mathbf{p}|\Delta \textbf{x},\Delta t] = \mathbf{p}_0 + \mathcal{O}(\Delta t), \qquad
    \mathbf{v}_0 = \gamma\,\mathbf{b}_0 + \beta\,\mathbf{p}_0
\end{equation}
which gives
\begin{align}
	\nonumber
    \partial_t P(\Delta \textbf{x},\Delta t) &= -\gamma\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}_\textbf{x}P -\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\cdot
    \left[\left(\textbf{v}_0 + \mathcal{O}(\Delta \textbf{x}) + \mathcal{O}(\Delta t)\right)P\right]\\
    \nonumber
    \partial_t P(\Delta \textbf{x},\Delta t) &= -\gamma\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}_\textbf{x}P - \mathbf{v}_0\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}P +  \left[\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^0) + \mathcal{O}(\Delta t^{1 - 1/\alpha})\right] \cdot P\\
    \partial_t \hat P(\mathbf{k},\Delta t) &= -\gamma\|\mathbf{k}\|^{\alpha}\hat P - i\,\mathbf{v}_0\cdot\mathbf{k}\,\hat P + \mathcal{O}(\Delta t^0) \hat{P}\\
    \implies \hat P(\mathbf{k},\Delta t) &= \exp\left[-\gamma\|\mathbf{k}\|^{\alpha}\Delta t - i\mathbf{v}_0\cdot\mathbf{k}\,\Delta t\right] [1 + \mathcal{O}(\Delta t)] \label{eq:characteristic_equation}
\end{align}
Showing that, to first order in $\Delta t$, the short-time diffusion under our model \cref{eq:fns_system} yields a $\mathcal{S}\alpha\mathcal{S}$ distribution, defined through its characteristic function, which is transported with velocity  $\mathbf{v}_0 = \gamma \textbf{b}_0 + \beta \textbf{p}_0$.

\subsubsection*{Quantification of Lévy superdiffusion monoscaling}
Following on from the solution for the characteristic equation $\hat{P}(\textbf{k},\Delta t) = \exp\left[-\gamma\|\mathbf{k}\|^{\alpha}\Delta t - i\mathbf{v}_0\cdot\mathbf{k}\,\Delta t\right] [1 + \mathcal{O}(\Delta t)]$, we reduce to the characteristic function of Fourier-space jump sized $k=\|\textbf{k}\|$. For $0<q<2$ this change of variables will give the relation \cite{matsui_fractional_2016}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fractional_moment_identity}
    \langle \| \Delta \mathbf{x} \|^q \rangle = C_q \int_0^\infty \left[ 1 - \mathrm{Re}\,\hat P(k,\Delta t) \right] k^{-1-q}\,\mathrm{d}k,
    \qquad C_q = \frac{q(1-q)}{\Gamma(2-q)\cos(q\pi/2)},
\end{equation}
where $C_q$ is defined at $q=1$ by its limit, $C_1 = 2/\pi$. Substituting our result of \cref{eq:characteristic_equation} into \cref{eq:fractional_moment_identity} and evaluating after a further change of variable $u = \gamma\,\Delta t\,k^{\alpha}$, we have for $0<q<\alpha$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fractional_moment_result}
    \langle \| \Delta \mathbf{x} \|^q \rangle = C_q\left[
        \frac{\Gamma\!\left(1-\frac{q}{\alpha}\right)}{q}\,(\gamma\Delta t)^{q/\alpha}
        + \frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{2-q}{\alpha}\right)}{2\alpha}\,\frac{v_0^2}{\gamma^{2}}\,(\gamma\Delta t)^{(2-\frac{2}{\alpha})+\frac{q}{\alpha}}
        + \mathcal{O}\!\left(\Delta t^{1+q/\alpha}\right)
    \right].
\end{equation}
where the first term is the moment of an $\alpha$-stable Lévy flight with scale $(\gamma\Delta t)^{1/\alpha}$, which is finite only for $q<\alpha$ \cite{samorodnitsky_stable_1994}. The second term is the ballistic contribution of the momentum, which will tend to dominate as oscillations over longer times. Since $2-\frac{2-q}{\alpha} > \frac{q}{\alpha}$ for $\alpha>1$, it is subleading at short times, affecting dynamics more as $q \rightarrow 0$ and $\alpha \rightarrow 1$. This gives diffusion through fractional absolute moments scaling as
\begin{equation}
    \langle \| \Delta \mathbf{x} \|^q \rangle \propto \Delta t^{\lambda(q)}, \qquad \lambda(q) = \frac{q}{\alpha}, \qquad 0<q<\alpha.
\end{equation}

Thus, for $q<\alpha$ the modelled dynamics of \cref{eq:fns_system} reproduce the scaling of a Lévy flight \cite{metzler_random_2000}. Such superdiffusion will produce a self-similar process with a single scaling exponent $1/\alpha > 1/2$ across all scales. This is called monoscaling superdiffusion, where superdiffusion is present and identical across all scales of $q$.

However, for $q\geq\alpha$ the moments of an unbounded Lévy flight diverge and the expansion above no longer applies. It is thus important to computationally examine the behaviour of a Lévy flight during exploration of particular potentials, whether or not they are unbounded or dynamic, and if any post-processing is done to handle divergences in the variance of jumps. In other studies, Lévy flights have been found to have $\lambda(q) \neq \frac{q}{\alpha}$, and are thus proposed to be multifractal, with different scaling behaviour for large jumps than for small ones \cite{dubkov_levy_2008, klafter_anomalous_2005}. This is the signature of strong anomalous diffusion \cite{castiglione_strong_1999}; however, it is misleading, as a Lévy flight is defined by monoscaling self-similarity. In this study, we find that under the confined dynamics and low-pass filtering to produce continuous trajectories, the Lévy flight yields a single monoscaling superdiffusive exponent of $\lambda(q) = \frac{q}{\alpha}$
