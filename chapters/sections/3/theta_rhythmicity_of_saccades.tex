\section{Theta rhythmicity of saccades}\label{sec:theta_rhythmicity_of_saccades}
The approximate \qty{200}{\ms} timescale of human saccades has long been understood, but their rhythmicity is not often quantified beyond this approximate scale \cite{meyer_rhythmicity_1966, saccadic_rhythm_1986, chota_rhythmic_2018, shaverdi_orchestration_2023}. Direct measures of saccade rhythmicity are found in the statistics of the distribution of inter-saccadic intervals (ISIs), the interval durations between saccades $\Delta t_i$. One such dimensionless measure is the variance of the ISI distribution in log-space. Using this, we can construct a measure called the rhythmicity index (RI):
\begin{equation}
	\text{RI} = \frac{1}{\text{CV}_{\ln}}, \qquad \text{CV}_{\ln} = \sqrt{e^{\sigma_{\ln}^2} - 1}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_{\ln}$ is the standard deviation of the distribution of the natural log of ISIs $\ln \Delta t$, with $\text{CV}_{\ln}$ often called the geometric CV. This $\text{RI}$ is an effective measure of the spread of a strictly positive distribution. A Poisson distributed variable will give $\text{RI}\approx 0.4891$\footnote{This can be found from the exponentially distributed intervals of a Poisson process with mean $\lambda$ as $X \sim \lambda e^{-\lambda X}$, changing variables to find the distribution of $\ln X = Y \sim \lambda \exp[ Y - \lambda \exp Y ]$. This distribution will have $\sigma_{\ln} = \frac{\pi}{\sqrt{6}} \approx 1.283$.}, and it is found that for an ISI distribution gathered from saccades across a disordered grid of dots is $\text{RI} = 2.69$ \cite{naher_primate_saccades}.

\subsection*{Strong rhythmicity of saccades with a slowly changing theta rhythm}
The recent discovery of the strength of saccade rhythmicity by \textcite{naher_primate_saccades} required an accounting of the variable rhythms of biological systems. A perfect clock does not drive the timescales of the brain, but instead is based on characteristic durations of certain regular patterns. As such, the \qty{200}{\milli \second} estimate for saccades during a visual search is not expected to be a universal timescale across subjects and trials. In the case of saccades, this rate can be found to vary within the theta-band of frequencies \qtyrange{4}{8}{\hertz} (see \cref{subsec:neur_freq_bands}). This slowly varying theta rate is expected to be stable within a single individual and a single trial; thus, we consider a local measure of rhythmicity based on the intervals between saccades, called inter-saccadic intervals (ISI). Such a metric can be proposed through the local rate-corrected rhythmicity index (RCRI) \cite{naher_primate_saccades}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:RCRI}
	\text{RCRI} = \frac{1}{1 + \langle \text{CV}_2 \rangle}, \qquad \text{CV}_2(i) = 2\frac{\|\Delta t_{i+1} - \Delta t_{i} \|}{\Delta t_{i+1} + \Delta t_{i}}
\end{equation}
which allows quantification of purely pairwise rhythmicity by comparing each interval $\Delta t_{i+1}$ to its predecessor $\Delta t_{i}$, and averaging across all intervals $i$ considered. To give baseline values, the $\text{RCRI}$ of a Poisson process is $\text{RCRI}=0.5$, and a perfectly rhythmic process will give $\text{RCRI}=1$. The use of RCRI as a local metric for rhythmicity has produced two results. Firstly, human saccades are very strongly rhythmic, with $\text{RCRI} \approx 0.765$ for saccades exploring a disordered grid of dots \cite{naher_primate_saccades}. Secondly, it has been found that the instantaneous saccade rate is a strong predictor for the local saccade rhythmicity $\text{RCRI}$, with a Pearson correlation found to be $\rho=0.39, P=0.001$, with saccade rate acting as a significant predictor of $\text{RCRI}$ with $\beta=\qty{0.0114}{\hertz^{-1}}, P=0.014$ \cite{naher_primate_saccades}. It has been found that those trials binned into the low-, medium-, and high- saccade rate tertiles will show a positive correlation of saccade rate with higher RCRI \cite{naher_primate_saccades}, as shown in \cref{fig:local_rhythmicity}\textbf{b)}. \subsubsection*{Interpretations of local rhythmicity and saccade rate for a model of gaze dynamics}
These results support our unified framework of gaze dynamics, specifically those predictions of the kernel dynamics described in \cref{subsec:adaptive_kernel_dynamics}. We have shown good agreement between the tight-gamma renewal fit to modelled ISIs and the empirical ISI statistics. We have also introduced a parameter $\tau$ with which to modulate the slowly varying theta-scale saccade rate, whose effects on rhythmicity and average saccade rate are shown in \cref{tab:theta_comparison}. A correlation between high saccade rate and high rhythmicity is predicted from the origin of regular dynamical-informational transitions as described in \cref{sec:dynamical-information_adaptation}, where a stronger kernel produces more rhythmic transitions, and a weaker kernel will approach an arrhythmic Poisson point distribution of ISIs, as fluctuation-driven exploration begins to dominate the search, reducing rhythmicity in saccades.
