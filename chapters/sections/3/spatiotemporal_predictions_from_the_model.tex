The above have been analyses of data which already exist in the literature to varying degrees of novelty. In particular, the approximate timescale of saccades has been known for the history of eye movement research, whereas the significance of the rate-corrected local rhythmicity is a very recent result only discovered in the year of this present work, 2026. Ocular microtremor as a phenomenon has been found in many studies, however consensus has never been reached as to it's importance, and it has never been linked to a computational role of gamma-scale rhythms. Lastly, the close mathematical analysis of search superdiffusion has only been recently undertaken, and where it has been studied it has been done naively as a simple Lévy walk with no awareness of the non-equilibrium potential landscape being explored. However, another benefit of comparing the generative unifying model presented in \cref{chap:a_unifying_model} to the data is that further dynamical predictions outside of the present literature can be made. Thus, we present experimental paradigms and features of this model which are being developed to better understand visual searching.
\subsection{Saliency-tremor frequency coupling in contrast-dependant search tasks}
That process which has been called ocular microtremor has been hypothesised to be harmonic oscillations in the control of the path of the eye during fixation tasks. An experiment may be designed in which targets of various degrees of contrast are presented to be searched. High contrast, localised points, such as high spatial frequency Gabor patches, would correlate with localised saliency wells. Whereas, low contrast, smeared low spatial frequency patches would correlate with delocalised saliency wells. High contrast wells would correspond to shorter theta timescales, quadratic in the effective saliency well size as in \cref{eq:theta_rhythm}, and a microtremor rhythm which is approximately inverse to the effective saliency well size as in \cref{eq:resonant_frequency}.
