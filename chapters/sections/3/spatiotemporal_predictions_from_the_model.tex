The above have been analyses of data which already exist in the literature to varying degrees of novelty. In particular, the approximate timescale of saccades has been known for the history of eye movement research, whereas the significance of the rate-corrected local rhythmicity is a very recent result only discovered in the year of this present work, 2026. Ocular microtremor as a phenomenon has been found in many studies, however consensus has never been reached as to it's importance, and it has never been linked to a computational role of gamma-scale rhythms. Lastly, the close mathematical analysis of search superdiffusion has only been recently undertaken, and where it has been studied it has been done naively as a simple Lévy walk with no awareness of the non-equilibrium potential landscape being explored. However, another benefit of comparing the generative unifying model presented in \cref{chap:a_unifying_model} to the data is that further dynamical predictions outside of the present literature can be made. Thus, we present experimental paradigms and features of this model which are being developed to better understand visual searching.
\subsection{Saliency-frequency coupling in contrast-dependant search tasks}
The ocular microtremor is here hypothesised to be harmonic oscillations in the control of the path of the eye during fixation tasks. An experiment may be designed in which targets with various degrees of contrast are presented to be searched. High contrast, localised points, such as tight, high spatial frequency Gabor patches, would produce localised saliency wells. Whereas, low contrast, large low spatial frequency patches would produce saliency wells with a large width $\sigma_{\text{fix}}$. High contrast wells would correspond to shorter theta timescales, quadratic in the effective saliency well size as in \cref{eq:theta_rhythm}, and a microtremor rhythm which is approximately inverse to the effective saliency well size as in \cref{eq:resonant_frequency}. It could this be examined if these dynamics are coupled, by experimentally saving trials with three data points: $t_{\theta}, f_{\text{peak}},$ and $k_{\text{Gabor}}$. A simple regression analysis can be achieved here to find correlations between spatial frequency $k_{\text{Gabor}}$ and the visual theta-rhythm $t_{\theta}$ and the $f_{\text{peak}}$. It can then also be tested if these effects are consistent with a potential well of variable size, such that $t_{\theta} \propto \sigma_{\text{fix}}^{-2}$ and $f \propto \sigma_{\text{fix}}^{-1}$
