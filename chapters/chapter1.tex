\chapter{Survey of the Literature}\label{chap:literature_review}
In this thesis, we aim to unify the rich gaze dynamics found during active visual search under a single physics-inspired model, admitting a deeper understanding of neural computation. There is growing success in turning methods from statistical physics and nonlinear dynamics towards the area of computational neuroscience. In particular, the rich and complex spatiotemporal activity of neural circuits inspires similar analysis to unify the dynamics of gaze trajectories across spatiotemporal scales, and present a physics-inspired dynamical understanding of neural computation in search tasks. 

In \cref{sec:gaze_traj}, we review the background for developing a scientific understanding of the dynamics of visual gaze; the data we present in this thesis, the unique spatiotemporal patterns of such data, and the previous methods used to model complex dynamics found in gaze trajectories are each described. In \cref{sec:diffusive_processes}, we introduce the theoretical framework of stochastic modelling, Lévy motion, and the anomalous superdiffusion found elsewhere in complex dynamical systems. \cref{sec:comp_sampling} then provides the link between stochastic dynamics and computation sampling, both in biological and abstract contexts; providing the key motivation for developing a mathematical understanding of a gaze trajectory as a proxy for dynamical attention in the brain.
