\chapter{Empirical Validations of Model Predictions}\label{chap:empirical_validations}
In order to evaluate and understand a scientific model, it is important to compare the current model to the given data, and with historically successful accounts of the physical system. In this chapter, we aim compare each of the individual modelled dynamics to their counterparts in data gathered by collaborators in \textcite{naher_primate_saccades}. In searching for validations of our mathematical model, we are faced with the question: which of the broad and often task-dependent dynamical patterns of eye movements are most important in developing our understanding of computation in the brain during visual search? We look to the broad literature of the computational effects of each component of eye movements, and find that our division of FNS dynamics maps well onto spatiotemporal signatures of eye movements which have been found and modelled elsewhere. In \cref{chap:a_unifying_model} a partitioning of FNS spatiotemporal dynamics was found to be useful: \ref{sec:dynamical-information_adaptation} dynamical-information transitional rhythmicity, \ref{sec:local_harmonic_oscillations} local high frequency harmonic oscillations, and \ref{sec:levy_superdiffusion} global spatial Lévy superdiffusion. In analogy, a high-level reviews of eye movement dynamics during decision-making tasks are examined as: 1. large theta-rhythmic macro- and microsaccades, 2. gamma-scale fixational ocular drifts, which have 3. global access to a visual informative scene. For such reviews of experimental findings of dynamical regimes of eye movements see \textcite{mahanama_eye_2022} and \textcite{martinez-conde_role_2004}


Furthermore, we compare the virtues of the presented model in unifying and predicting multiscale spatiotemporal dynamics of eye movements. Although there has been important work in empirical studies of saccadic eye movements \cite{naher_primate_saccades,ludwig_mechanism_2009, erkelens_coordination_2006, rucci_control_2015} and fixations \cite{otero-millan_fixational_2014, martinez-conde_role_2004, graham_ocular_2023, ko_eye_2016}, it is rare that the combined dynamics of large-scale and short-scale eye movements are considered experimentally. Similarly, modelling studies have made a focus of creating saccade prediction \cite{kammerer2016deepgaze2, Kummerer2022_DeepGazeIII, cornia_predicting_2018, lencastre_dynamical_2025}, otherwise the focus is generally in modelling noise signatures of fixational activity without physical or biological motivation \cite{vasudevan_stochastic_nodate, kuang_temporal_2012, engbert_integrated_2011, NIPS2017_194cf6c2}. In the present chapter, we present those predictions from the model presented in \cref{chap:a_unifying_model} serve to unify these various experimental and modelling studies to capture key dynamics of eye movements which are important in visual searching. The explanation of functional roles these dynamics play during informative searching will be presented in the following \cref{chap:functional_advantages}.
