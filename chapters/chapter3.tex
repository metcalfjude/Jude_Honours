\chapter{Empirical Validations}\label{chap:empirical_validations}
In this chapter, we evaluate the predictions of the model developed in \cref{chap:a_unifying_model} against high precision eye-tracking data from \textcite{naher_primate_saccades}, described in \cref{sec:model_setup}. Motivated by established spatiotemporal signatures of gaze during visual search, we examine three key dynamical features: (i) theta-rhythmic saccades, (ii) gamma-scale \qtyrange{40}{100}{\hertz} fixational ocular drift, and (iii) global superdiffusion across spatial scales \cite{mahanama_eye_2022, martinez-conde_role_2004}. Our unifying framework seeks to explain these phenomena through network-level neural activity, including the proposed connection between OMT and neural gamma, theta-gamma coupling, and the monoscaling of superdiffusion across spatial scales.

\Cref{sec:theta_rhythmicity_of_saccades} examines theta rhythmicity of saccades, comparing the model's transitional rhythmicity with the saccade timing in the data. \cref{sec:gamma_oscillations} examines gamma-scale oscillations during visual fixations, testing the model's local high-frequency harmonic oscillations against fixational drift. \cref{sec:search_superdiffusion} examines superdiffusion in search, assessing whether the global spatial statistics of gaze are consistent with Lévy superdiffusion. Finally, \cref{sec:spatiotemporal_predictions} presents the model's spatiotemporal predictions across these scales and discusses how they unify the experimental and modelling results presented above.

\subsection*{Summary}
Taken together, this work has validated our physics-inspired framework for mapping gaze to human eye-tracking data. We provide a mechanistic explanation of the recently discovered significant rhythmicity of saccades during search, attributed to a coupling between model dynamics and information via kernel deposition. We then predicted gamma-scale oscillations in fixation dynamics and compared these to the OMT frequency in human gaze. This component of fixational motion is rarely studied as a coherent aspect of gaze; we provide a mechanistic explanation of its origin. Finally, we presented the fluctuation-driven superdiffusion in model dynamics across all spatial scales. By analysing fractional $q$--moments, we found that gaze superdiffusion is characterised by a single exponent across all scales, suggesting a unification of the dynamics of fixations and saccades under a single model. Finally, we suggest experimental methods based on model predictions to examine visual attention via gaze.
