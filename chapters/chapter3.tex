\chapter{Empirical Validations}\label{chap:empirical_validations}
Our model is designed to be compared against empirical data and against established accounts of the phenomenon it describes, rich gaze dynamics during visual search. In this chapter, we compare each of the modelled dynamics of \cref{chap:a_unifying_model} with high precision eye-tracking data gathered by collaborators in \textcite{naher_primate_saccades} as described in \cref{sec:model_setup}. gaze dynamics during visual search show many broad and often task-dependent spatiotemporal patterns, so we ask which are most important for understanding computation in the brain. The literature inspires our partition of modelled dynamics, namely \ref{sec:dynamical-information_adaptation} information rhythmicity, \ref{sec:local_harmonic_oscillations} local short-time oscillatory sampling, and \ref{sec:levy_superdiffusion} global spatial superdiffusion, maps onto important visual dynamical signatures that have been observed elsewhere. These correspond to (1) theta-rhythmic macrosaccades, (2) gamma-scale \qtyrange{40}{100}{\hertz} fixational ocular drift, and (3) global saccadic superdiffusion to an informative visual scene \cite{mahanama_eye_2022, martinez-conde_role_2004}. Importantly, our unifying model provides an understand of each of these dynamics as a function of network-level neural activity, including the novel connection between microtremor and the neural gamma signal, theta-gamma coupling, and the monoscaling of Lévy superdiffusion across spatial scales.

\cref{sec:theta_rhythmicity_of_saccades} examines theta rhythmicity in saccades, comparing the model's transitional rhythmicity with the saccade timing in the data. \cref{sec:gamma_oscillations} examines gamma-scale oscillations during visual fixations, testing the model's local high-frequency harmonic oscillations against fixational drift. \cref{sec:search_superdiffusion} examines superdiffusion in search, assessing whether the global spatial statistics of gaze are consistent with Lévy superdiffusion. Finally, \cref{sec:spatiotemporal_predictions} presents the spatiotemporal predictions of the model across these scales and discusses how they unify the experimental and modelling findings above.

\section*{Summary}
After examining our proposed model in depth, we have now compared to real human gaze data. This work has provided a dynamical explanation of the recently discovered significant regularity of saccades during a search task as a coupling between model dynamics and information through kernel deposition. We then made the novel prediction of gamma-scale oscillations in fixation dynamics, which was validated under the present data analysis. This component of fixational dynamics has rarely been studied as a coherent aspect of neural dynamics; we are the first to provide a modelled explanation of its neural origin. Finally, we presented the prediction of our model of unified fluctuation-driven superdiffusive motion across all spatial scales. By analysing fractional $q$--moments, we found that gaze trajectories are characterised by a single superdiffusive exponent across all scales, unifying the dynamics of fixations and of saccades under a single model. Finally, we suggest further experimental methods to test further quantitative predictions of the model designed to examine attention in the brain through the study of gaze dynamics.
