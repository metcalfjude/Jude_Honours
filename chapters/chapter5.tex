\chapter{Discussion}\label{chap:discussion}

% 1. Summary
In this thesis we have presented the first model to unify the rich multiscale dynamics of visual search. Previous work has been focused between the two distinct scales of gaze dynamics: the slow timescales and large spatial scales of saccades, or the fast timescales and fine spatial scales of fixation in gaze. In this thesis we unify these scales under a single physics-inspired theory. Further, motivations for previous work on gaze dynamics has also been divided in either aiming to present a biologically accurate understanding of visual search, or a full mechanistic generative model of eye-tracking data. Taking aim to unify these motivations, we constructed a predictive model from the neural dynamics developed under the theory of fractional neural sampling (FNS) \cite{qi_fractional_2022, chen_spatiotemporal_2022}, and present methods and empirical studies which can fit real human data to the theory. 

%Under this framework, gaze dynamics are governed by a second-order stochastic process (\cref{eq:fns_system}) driven by Lévy noise exploring a dynamical saliency landscape adapts through a dynamical-informative kernel (\cref{sec:dynamical-information_adaptation}). We understand the model dynamics through three key spatiotemporal signatures: global $\alpha$--Lévy superdiffusion, rhythmic transitions between potential wells driven by an adaptation from a kernel function $k(\cdot)$, and local high-frequency harmonic oscillations driven by a memory--momentum parameter $\beta$. Each of these signatures maps onto a well-known behaviour during gaze dynamics. We compared each of these dynamics with the high-resolution gaze data of \textcite{naher_primate_saccades} and with the wider literature, and found that the model captures the signature spatiotemporal dynamics of saccades and fixations within a single framework (\cref{chap:empirical_validations}). In particular, we discovered robust monoscaling Lévy superdiffusion on all spatial scales with $\alpha = 1.12$, theta-rhythmic saccades with $t_{\theta} \approx \qty{200}{\milli \second}$ and a high degree of rhythmicity $\text{RCRI}=0.72$, and local high-frequency harmonic oscillations with mean frequency $f=\qty{56.5}{\hertz}$. Finally, after validating the biologically-motivated model of gaze dynamics, we formulated functional explanations for each of their existence. Using information-theoretic metrics on the updating belief distribution present in the model, we show that each pattern plays a distinct functional role in the efficiency of informative search (\cref{chap:functional_advantages}). 

% 2. The novel physics
The methods and dynamics here studied are of importance to physicists as an applied description of a non-equilibrium complex dynamical system. First, visual dynamics are shown to be superdiffusive, and moreover display robust monoscaling, governed by a single stability index $\alpha$. Such anomalous diffusion at every scale is of interest when studying how basic computational dynamics may be constructed from complex dynamical systems at criticality. We characterised the short-time diffusive scaling of eye-tracking data through fractional absolute moments (\cref{sec:search_superdiffusion}), and compared these to the monoscaling superdiffusion of Lévy dynamics (\cref{sec:levy_superdiffusion}). Such rich multiscale self-similarity provides a physical explanation as the result of control from a neuronal network at criticality with diverging correlation length, resulting in nonlocal spatial access to an informative landscape \cite{Meshulam_coarse_2019, santo_scalefree_2018, Beggs_criciality_2022, tkacik_critical_2015, cocchi_critical_2017}. 

Second, the model presents and explains the unique pattern of nested oscillations: the slow theta-scale rhythmic transitions between regions of interest, which modulate fast gamma-scale local sampling dynamics, arising from the broad patterns of neural computation, and the confinement of a sampling process. Nested timescales are a recurrent feature of coherent neural dynamics, especially during communication of information between areas of the brain \cite{tan_theta_2024, spyropoulos_theta_2018, fries_mechanism_2005, bastos_communication_2015, fries_rhythms_2015, fries_rhythmic_2023}. The present model carries this framework over to gaze dynamics, as they emerge from the same neural dynamics which control the process of active vision. 

Third, the dynamical-informative kernel introduced in this work describes how visual dynamics are influenced by the history of the gaze trajectory. In particular the informational efficiency of gaze trajectories and the phenomenon of inhibition of return (IoR) suggest a strong dependance on the informative history of a search. Our formulation naturally encodes this information through a belief landscape which evolves from a prior saliency $\pi_0$ to a posterior belief after exploration $\pi_t$. The cumulative information gathered is then well defined through the Kullback--Leibler (KL--) divergence. Such a well-motivated physics-inspired development of interpretable memory-effects, heavy-tailed noise, and nonlocality is important in the development and progress of physics as an interdisciplinary field.

% 3. Importance for neuroscience
This work advances the study of the brain in neuroscience, as our model turns gaze into a source of interpretable parameters modulating neural attention, a very important topic of study when understanding decision-making in the brain. The active parameter space here defined through the stability index $\alpha$, memory--momentum strength $\beta$, noise scale $\gamma$, and informative timescale $t_{\theta}$: each parameter has a well-understood and motivated interpretation in neural dynamics. Because gaze aggregates the activity of many brain regions, designing experimental paradigms to fit parameters and summarise collective properties of the circuits that control and process visual input is very impactful to take non-invasive measurement of the brain.

An experimental protocol tracking high-frequency eye movement alongside coarse-grained saccade positions during a standardised search task without head restraint, combined with head acceleration measurements, would resolve gamma-scale fixational dynamics in naturalistic, head-free viewing and separate eye from head contributions. Such an experiment is simple to design using new low-cost and non-invasive techniques such as described in \cite{ryle_simultaneous_2015} and the data extracted is tractable to analyse across the scales of interest here, to test the predictions of FNS dynamics here presented.

% 4. Importance and application to machine learning
The same sampling process applies to machine learning, in particular to sampling accurately from a given posterior distribution $\pi_{\text{post}}(\bm{\theta})$ over a set of parameters $\bm{\theta}$, a problem often handled through the use Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampling. Standard MCMC methods are based on sampling a chain of points in parameter space, and proposing local moves through a probability landscape $V(\bm{\theta}) = -\ln \pi_{\text{post}}(\bm{\theta})$, often incorporating momentum-coupled Langevin dynamics through parameter space as introduced in \cref{sec:diffusive_processes}. Such dynamics often are unable to robustly explore and mix between well-separated modes, and designing an algorithm to robustly explore a wide range of unseen heavy-tailed or multimodal distributions efficiently without fine-tuning of algorithmic hyperparameters remains a long-standing and active topic in machine learning \cite{neal_sampling_1996, latuszynski_mcmc_2025, speagle_conceptual_2020}. The superdiffusive sampling of this thesis address this issue directly, as we have found that in an unseen search task, superdiffusive jumps will efficiently gather informative samples. The momentum-driven local oscillations have also been shown to improve sensitivity to sampled information and efficient sample mixing, as found in similar momentum-driven dynamics in Hamiltonian Monte Carlo \cite{betancourt_conceptual_2018, Ottobre2016-xp} and second-order Langevin sampling \cite{liu2025secondorderensemblelangevin, Ottobre2016-xp}. Further, the adaptive kernel resembles well-tempered metadynamics \cite{tiwary_time-independent_2015}, in which deposited bias discourages revisiting explored regions, robustly improving ergodicity without reliance on parameter tuning on the unknown landscape $V(\bm{\theta})$. The link to free-energy minimisation and active inference \cite{NIPS2017_194cf6c2, tiwary_time-independent_2015, bussi_using_2020, Friston2010-aa, parr_active_2022} is important when formalising this link, since both theories consider search as a minimisation of uncertainty about a landscape. 

Our results emphasise that coupling oscillations across scales is itself important for an abstract computational sampler: rapid local oscillations explore each mode, while slower rhythmic transitions decide when to move on. Superdiffusive dynamics in generalised computational systems have recently attracted considerable interest, both from theoretical studies of such dynamics emerging from informational sampling \cite{sandev_anomalous_2026, oliveira_anomalous_2019, timashev_anomalous_2009, chen_anomalous_2022} as well as in designing algorithms in MCMC sampling of abstract distribution landscapes \cite{simsekli_fractional_nodate, ijcai2018p419, latuszynski_mcmc_2025, oechsler_levy_2024, behme_levy_2026}. The same principle of heavy-tailed nonlocality in computation has also influenced more general AI frameworks, notably in work from our group on Fractional Neural Attention (FNA) \cite{qu2025fractionalneuralattentionefficient}. FNA is designed to token interactions through Lévy diffusion, as governed by the fractional Laplacian, so that short- and long-range dependencies arise from a single operator rather than hand-designed local--global patterns or fine-tuned scales of influence. In the reported experiments it matches or exceeds Transformer baselines on text classification, image classification, and machine translation, in some cases with a single layer, and its attention networks show larger spectral gaps and shorter path lengths, consistent with faster information mixing. That the same mechanism describes both gaze and attention in artificial networks supports the view that multiscale, nonlocal sampling is a general computational strategy.

% 5. Broad conclusions
In conclusion, this thesis has shown that the rich and apparently disparate dynamics of human gaze can be described by a single physical model with a small set of interpretable components which derive from neural dynamics: nonlocal Lévy jumps, nested oscillations, and an adaptive, information-gathering search landscape. Gaze is both easily measured and serves as a strong proxy for neural decision-making and attention, so gaining an understanding of gaze dynamics gives access to basic computation in the brain. The model offers a quantitative framework making use of concepts from the statistical physics of diffusive processes, makes testable predictions for new experiments to test the theory, and provides a process through which we can understand neural attention at a large scale, and interpret it to develop and understand brain health and basic function of abstract dynamical computational systems.
