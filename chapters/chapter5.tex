\chapter{Discussion}\label{chap:discussion}

The important contribution of our physics-inspired model is that it unifies and predicts eye-movement dynamics across scales. Empirical work has examined saccades \cite{naher_primate_saccades, ludwig_mechanism_2009, erkelens_coordination_2006, rucci_control_2015} and fixations \cite{otero-millan_fixational_2014, martinez-conde_role_2004, graham_ocular_2023, ko_eye_2016}, but rarely considers large- and small-scale movements together. Modelling studies have likewise focused either on saccade prediction \cite{kammerer2016deepgaze2, kummerer_deepgaze_2022, cornia_predicting_2018, lencastre_dynamical_2025} or on noise signatures of fixational activity that lack physical or biological motivation \cite{vasudevan_stochastic_nodate, kuang_temporal_2012, engbert_integrated_2011, NIPS2017_194cf6c2}. We show that the predictions of \cref{chap:a_unifying_model} bring these strands together; their functional roles in informative search are addressed in \cref{chap:functional_advantages}.

Developing models of gaze dynamics during search tasks has been challenging, as eye movements consist of various rich dynamics which are nontrivial to describe. In particular, eye movements during visual search are marked by nonlocal jump dynamics, known as saccades, statistically regular periods and locations of fixation, as well as rich dynamics which aid perception during fixations. An understanding of active perception through active vision must be developed through unifying the many dynamics which have been discovered and which have found functional roles in active vision. Models which focus on saccade prediction \cite{huang_salicon_2015, bremer_predicting_2021, cornia_predicting_2018, kittler_effective_2026}, global statistical search strategies \cite{NIPS2017_194cf6c2, blazejczyk_stochastic_2021,lencastre_dynamical_2025}, and characterising fixational noise \cite{engbert_integrated_2011, kummerer_understanding_2017, vasudevan_stochastic_nodate} have all been valuable in understanding an understanding of the dynamics of the eye across many scales individually. The aim of this chapter is instead to capture all of the functionally important dynamics of visual searching under a single physical theory, which accounts for top-to-bottom neural control and visual perception. This approach is made possible largely in the development of the theory of fractional neural sampling (FNS) to describe dynamics of travelling waves of neural activity in \textcite{qi_fractional_2022}. A theoretically motivated characterisation of FNS and its applications to visual dynamics was presented in \textcite{chen_spatiotemporal_2022}, although that study does not aim towards developing a realistic model of eye movements. 

The second development has been the collaboration with the authors \textcite{naher_primate_saccades} towards developing FNS dynamics to describe empirically-understood rich features of gaze dynamics. This group have collected and analysed spatiotemporal features of high resolution empirical data of human gaze trajectories during search tasks, as described in \cref{sec:model_setup}. A scientific model is important only insofar as it explains, replicates, and predicts data, and thus much emphasis has been made in comparing to these and other results closely. In particular, this collaboration inspired the introduction of a dynamical-informational adaptation to a searched visual scene, as presented in \cref{sec:dynamical-information_adaptation}. This adaptation was initially developed to account for the recently discovered rhythmicity in wait-times between saccades, as discussed further in \cref{sec:theta_rhythmicity_of_saccades}, but came to capture the link between dynamics and an informational gathering capacity of the model, as described in \cref{sec:information-theoretic_metrics}.
