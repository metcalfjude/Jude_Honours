\chapter{Discussion}\label{chap:discussion}

% 1. Summary
In this thesis we have presented the first model to unify the rich multiscale dynamics of visual search. Previous models have focused their object of study on large-scale saccades or small-scale fixations, and motivations for such models are divided in either aiming to present a biologically accurate understanding of visual search, or a black-box description of gaze dynamics as in machine learning. Taking aim to unify these aims and objects of study, we constructed a model from fractional neural sampling (FNS) \cite{qi_fractional_2022, chen_spatiotemporal_2022}, in which gaze is represented through a second-order stochastic process \ref{eq:fns_system} driven by Lévy noise in a saliency landscape that adapts through a dynamical-informative kernel (\cref{chap:a_unifying_model}). We understand these dynamics through three key signatures: global Lévy superdiffusion, theta-rhythmic transitions between regions of interest, and local high-frequency harmonic oscillations within each region. We compared each with the high-resolution gaze data of \textcite{naher_primate_saccades} and with the wider literature, and found that the model captures the characteristic spatiotemporal signatures of saccades and fixations within a single framework (\cref{chap:empirical_validations}). Finally, we used information-theoretic metrics to show that each pattern plays a distinct functional role in the efficiency of informative search (\cref{chap:functional_advantages}). % TODO: add one sentence stating key and broad quantitative results.

% 2. The novel physics
Three ingredients of the model are of general interest in physics. First, the superdiffusion is monoscaling, governed by a single stability index $\alpha$. We characterised its short-time, noise-driven scaling of fractional absolute moments and the limits of monofractality that such a description can represent, which sets the boundary between what a single exponent can explain and what would require richer multiscaling. Second, the model contains nested oscillations: theta-scale rhythmic transitions between regions, and gamma-scale harmonic oscillations within them, arising from the memory--momentum term and the confining potential. Nested timescales are a recurrent feature of neural dynamics \cite{tan_theta_2024, spyropoulos_theta_2018}, and here they emerge from the same stochastic equation rather than being imposed separately. Third, the dynamical-informative kernel makes the potential history-dependent, so the searcher deposits kernel as it samples and its belief landscape evolves from a prior $\pi_0$ to a posterior $\pi_t$. The cumulative information gathered is then well defined as a Kullback--Leibler divergence. Together these describe an open, history-dependent, noise-driven system exploring an adaptive landscape, and they give gaze a quantitative language for memory, heavy-tailed fluctuations, and characteristic timescales.

% 3. Importance for neuroscience
For neuroscience, the model turns gaze into a source of interpretable parameters. The stability index, memory--momentum strength, informative timescale, and kernel strength each have a defined effect on the trajectory, so fitting them to data estimates the dynamics of the underlying attentional control. Several experiments would sharpen this. High-frequency eye tracking without a bite-bar, combined with head acceleration measurements, would resolve gamma-scale fixational dynamics in naturalistic, head-free viewing and separate eye from head contributions. High-quality smooth pursuit experiments, in which the target moves, would probe how the landscape is tracked over time. Dynamical search tasks with controlled likelihood landscapes would constrain the kernel and the theta-rhythmic transitions. Because gaze aggregates the activity of many regions, the fitted parameters may summarise collective properties of the circuits that control and process visual input. A natural candidate is the interaction between the superior colliculus, which integrates sensory, cortical, and goal-related signals into a map that drives saccades \cite{[CITE]}, and the visual cortex, which supplies the evidence on which that map is built \cite{[CITE]}. Within the active vision loop, the model's evolving potential could be compared with such a priority map, and the kernel with processes such as inhibition of return \cite{ludwig_mechanism_2009, redden_inhibition_2021}, giving a way to study attention in this system quantitatively. Because alterations of eye movements accompany many neurological and psychiatric conditions \cite{[CITE]}, mechanistic parameters of this kind also offer a route to more accurate biomarkers, and potentially to treatments that target a specific part of the loop.

% 4. Importance and application to machine learning
The same ideas apply to machine learning, in particular to sampling from multimodal and heavy-tailed distributions. Standard Markov chain Monte Carlo methods based on local moves struggle to cross between well-separated modes. The model's superdiffusive jumps address this directly, and its adaptive kernel resembles well-tempered metadynamics \cite{tiwary_time-independent_2015}, in which deposited bias discourages revisiting explored regions. The link to free-energy minimisation and active inference \cite{NIPS2017_194cf6c2, [CITE]} is also close, since both treat search as the reduction of uncertainty about a landscape. Our results emphasise that coupling oscillations across scales is itself important for an abstract sampler: rapid local oscillations explore each mode, while slower rhythmic transitions decide when to move on. Superdiffusive samplers have recently attracted considerable interest \cite{[CITE]}. The same principle has influenced more general AI frameworks, notably in work from our group on fractional neural attention (FNA) \cite{qu_fractional_2025}. FNA models token interactions through L\'evy diffusion governed by the fractional Laplacian, so that short- and long-range dependencies arise from a single operator rather than hand-designed local--global patterns. In the reported experiments it matches or exceeds Transformer baselines on text classification, image classification, and machine translation, in some cases with a single layer, and its attention networks show larger spectral gaps and shorter path lengths, consistent with faster information mixing. That the same mechanism describes both gaze and attention in artificial networks supports the view that multiscale, nonlocal sampling is a general computational strategy.

% 5. Broad conclusions
In conclusion, this thesis has shown that the rich and apparently disparate dynamics of human gaze can be described by a single physical model with a small set of interpretable ingredients: nonlocal L\'evy jumps, nested oscillations, and an adaptive, information-gathering landscape. Gaze is both easily measured and strongly linked to neural decision-making, so understanding its dynamics gives access to basic computation in the brain, namely how it samples, remembers, and decides under uncertainty. The model offers a quantitative framework for this, makes testable predictions for new experiments, and links vision to wider questions in statistical physics and artificial intelligence. Much remains to be done, in particular multiscaling beyond a single exponent, direct tests in neural recordings, and clinical application, but we hope the framework provides a firm basis for this work.
