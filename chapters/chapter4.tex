\chapter{Functional advantages of spatiotemporal searching dynamics}\label{chap:functional_advantages}
The power of a well-motivated biologically realistic model of eye movements is in examining functional roles of individual dynamical components studied in the rich task of gathering information. This approach allows an understanding of brain function in the higher layer of network-level neural sampling during visual search. A major motivation for this study is the previous work in the information efficiency of the brain; firstly, that movements are well predicted by greedy informational-maximisation strategies \cite{Renninger2005-ee, zhu_autocorrelated_2024, butko_infomax_2010}, and secondly that this efficiency and function of gaze dynamics during search is a significant indicator of brain health and function \cite{papanikolaou_levy_2024, caldani_saccadic_2017, morita_eye_2017, zhu_diagnosis_2024, lindner_disorders_2005, bittencourt_saccadic_2013, wang_saccadic_2025}. This forms a novel connection between the abstract performance of probabilistic sampling and the health and function of visual search performance as a proxy for network-level neurological functioning. Each dynamical component here described has analogy to a feature of abstract sampling algorithms: kernel-driven adaptation will improve ergodicity and can be compared to metadynamics \cite{tiwary_time-independent_2015,bussi_using_2020,barducci_well-tempered_2008}, local harmonic oscillations can be compared to the fast mixing and negative autocorrelation in sampling found in Hamiltonian Monte Carlo \cite{betancourt_conceptual_2018, Ottobre2016-xp} and second-order Langevin sampling methods \cite{liu2025secondorderensemblelangevin, Ottobre2016-xp}, and finally Lévy superdiffusion has gathered a growing body of literature as a robust sampling strategy which does not require fine-tuning to explore unknown heavy-tailed or multimodal distributions \cite{simsekli_fractional_nodate, ijcai2018p419, oechsler_levy_2024, behme_levy_2026}.

The first section of this chapter (\cref{sec:functional_roles_of_dynamical_patterns}) presents informational-theoretic methods which have been constructed to analyse efficiency during abstract search tasks. Key to this method is the quantification of information of a belief distribution as the distribution entropy $S_{\pi_t} = -\int_{\textbf{x}} d\textbf{x} \ln \pi_t(\textbf{x})$, and tracking its development through a search task. The second section (\cref{sec:functional_roles_of_dynamical_patterns}) takes these metrics and compares the informational advantages from dynamics driven in parameter space $\alpha/\beta/\gamma/\tau$. The final section (\cref{sec:further_extensions_of_studied_dynamic}) examines further extensions of these dynamics beyond the case of 2-dimensional visual search to high-dimensional or dynamical target landscapes, where control and exploration are hypothesised be more responsive and robust to shifts in a loss function.
