\chapter{Functional Advantages of Visual Search Dynamics}\label{chap:functional_advantages}
Now that we have developed and validated a physics-inspired model of gaze that accounts for information gathering, we investigate what we can learn from the model's efficiency in an abstract search task. The first section of this chapter (\cref{sec:functional_roles_of_dynamical_patterns}) presents information-theoretic methods that have been developed to analyse efficiency in abstract search. This builds on the framework introduced in \cref{chap:a_unifying_model}, using information encoded in kernel dynamics, quantified via belief distribution entropy. We take these metrics and assess the informational advantages of the two key dynamical patterns studied throughout this thesis: superdiffusion and nested dual-scale oscillations. This is done by comparing an abstract sampler operating at different points in $\alpha/\beta$--parameter space. The final section of the main body of this thesis (\cref{sec:further_extensions_of_studied_dynamic}) examines further extensions of these dynamics beyond the case of a 2-dimensional visual search to high-dimensional or dynamical target landscapes, where studied dynamics are hypothesised to promote a robust and responsive strategy to changing or complex goals, inspired by the computation of the brain.

\subsection*{Summary}

In this chapter, we treated our gaze model as an abstract sampler and asked how superdiffusion and momentum-driven oscillations contribute to search efficiency. Information gathering was quantified through the KL divergence $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_t \,\|\, \pi_0)$ between the evolving belief and the prior belief, and summarised the informational efficiency by three metrics: first-passage time (FPT), information velocity (IV), and shape exponent (SE). Examining the joint effects and influence of $\alpha$ and $\beta$ on search performance yields two main results. Momentum coupling $\beta$ correlates significantly with IV and SE ($\rho_{\mathrm{IV}} = 0.64$--$0.77$, $\rho_{\mathrm{SE}} = 0.51$--$0.81$), with a weaker effect on FPT. Lower $\alpha$ improved all three metrics, most strongly IV ($\rho_{\mathrm{IV}} = -0.75$ to $-0.95$). The two effects are then found to be coupled. Near Brownian noise, performance is optimised for $\beta$ near the oscillation boundary, whereas at low $\alpha$ this ridge broadens and the search becomes monotonic in $\beta$. We therefore interpret superdiffusion as providing global access to the landscape and momentum as providing local refinement, which together give good performance without fine-tuning. We then outlined two extensions, a decaying kernel for time-varying goals and a superdiffusive MCMC sampler, as proposals for future work.
